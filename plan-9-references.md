# plan9 — Annotated Bibliography

Every source used in `plan-9-one-pager.md`, `plan-9-design.md`, and `plan-9-operating-agreement.md`, with what it supports and honest ratings.

## Rating keys

**Evidence strength** — `E1` peer-reviewed causal (experiment / natural experiment / IV) · `E2` peer-reviewed meta-analysis or review · `E3` peer-reviewed correlational (panel/survey/qualitative) · `E4` peer-reviewed or foundational theory (no data) · `E5` primary legal authority (statute / precedential case) · `E6` agency or university official guidance · `E7` established practitioner / industry-standard document or data provider · `E8` practitioner explainer / compiled secondary / anecdote.

**Verification level** (required disclosure — this work was produced in a sandbox whose egress proxy returned HTTP 403 for most full-page fetches):
- **[F]** — full text fetched and read this session (GitHub-hosted primary documents/mirrors; mirror-fidelity caveats noted).
- **[S]** — URL verified live via search engine this session: the URL, title, and content excerpt were returned by the search index, confirming the page exists and previewing its content, but full text was not fetched. Quoted mechanics come from returned excerpts, not memory.
- **[U]** — unverified lead; named for completeness, never load-bearing.

No URL below was constructed from memory. Counsel and founders should pull primaries before executing documents.

## A. Founder equity splits — negotiation, structure, outcomes

1. **Hellmann, T., & Wasserman, N. (2017). "The First Deal: The Division of Founder Equity in New Ventures." *Management Science* 63(8), 2647–2666.** — [journal](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2474) [S]; [NBER w16922](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16922) [S]; [open PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w16922/w16922.pdf) [S]. *Supports:* 32% equal splits; 73% split in first month; quick-equal splits → lower financing likelihood and valuations; ~$450K NPV at stake; only ~10% adopt milestone vesting (design §§I.1, I.4). *Strength:* E3 — flagship journal, 511-venture survey panel; correlational, authors disclaim clean causality.
2. **Mueller, E., & Hennicke, M. (2024). "Unequal implies success?…" *European Management Journal* 42(3).** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0263237322001785) [S]. *Supports:* unequal splits → +1.3pp 3-yr growth in 24,194 German startups (§I.1). *Strength:* E3 — large registry panel with mediation analysis; correlational; different institutional context (Germany).
3. **Kagan, E., Leider, S., & Lovejoy, W. S. (2020). "Equity Contracts and Incentive Design in Start-Up Teams." *Management Science* 66(10).** — [journal](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3439) [S]; [SSRN version](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=3037444) [S]. *Supports:* equal splits attract free-riders/least-desirable types; contingent + delayed contracting improves outcomes — the causal backbone of the dynamic-ledger choice (§§I.1, I.4). *Strength:* E1 — lab experiment; causal within lab; external validity to real ventures is the standard caveat.
4. **Wasserman, N., practitioner summary, "Splitting the Pie: Founding-Team Equity Splits."** — [noamwasserman.com](https://www.noamwasserman.com/splitting-the-pie-founding-team-equity-splits/) [S]. *Supports:* time-based vesting as "weak proxy for value creation" (§I.4). *Strength:* E7 (author summarizing his own E3 research).
5. **Carta, "Founder equity split trends 2024."** — [carta.com](https://carta.com/data/founder-equity-split-trends-2024/) [S]. *Supports:* counter-trend — equal splits rising (2-founder: 31.5%→45.9%, 2015–2024) (§I.1 counter-evidence). *Strength:* E7 — large cap-table data provider; descriptive.

## B. Fairness and justice inside founding teams

6. **Breugst, N., Patzelt, H., & Rathgeber, P. (2015). "How should we divide the pie?…" *Journal of Business Venturing* 30(1), 66–94.** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883902614000676) [S]. *Supports:* perceived justice of the split (not equality) drives team spirals (§I.2). *Strength:* E3 — qualitative, 8 teams over 6 months; theory-building.
7. **Weissenböck, E., Breugst, N., Patzelt, H., & Dibbern, R. (2024). "Equity ownership and identification with the founding team." *Applied Psychology*.** — [Wiley](https://iaap-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/apps.12515) [S]. *Supports:* inverted-U between stake and team identification — why the design avoids zeroing anyone out (§I.2, §II.5). *Strength:* E3 — multi-wave survey; correlational.
8. **Sapienza, H. J., & Korsgaard, M. A. (1996). "Procedural Justice in Entrepreneur–Investor Relations." *AMJ* 39(3).** — [AOM](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/256655) [S]. *Supports:* procedural fairness (voice, timely information) builds trust/commitment — the transparency requirements (§I.2, §II.2.3). *Strength:* E3 — classic; mixed design.

## C. Contribution measurement, free-riding, monitoring

9. **Karau, S. J., & Williams, K. D. (1993). "Social loafing: A meta-analytic review…" *JPSP* 65(4).** — [open PDF](http://www.psych.purdue.edu/~willia55/392F-'06/KarauWilliamsMetaAnalysisJPSP.pdf) [S]. *Supports:* loafing robust across 78 studies; eliminated by identifiability/evaluability — the artifact-linked logging rule (§I.3, §II.2.3). *Strength:* E2/E1 — meta-analysis of experiments; causal at mechanism level.
10. **Erez, A., LePine, J. A., & Elms, H. (2002). "Effects of rotated leadership and peer evaluation…" *Personnel Psychology* 55(4).** — [record](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/effects-of-rotated-leadership-and-peer-evaluation-on-the-function/) [S]. *Supports:* peer evaluation → better sharing/performance; rotated facilitation benign (§I.3, §II.7). *Strength:* E1(quasi) — 38 student self-managed teams; modest external validity, honestly flagged where used.
11. **Alchian, A. A., & Demsetz, H. (1972). "Production, Information Costs, and Economic Organization." *AER* 62(5).** — [open PDF](https://josephmahoney.web.illinois.edu/BA549_Fall%202010/Session%205/Alchian_Demsetz%20(1972).pdf) [S]. *Supports:* metering problem → shirking; monitoring tied to residual claims (§I.3). *Strength:* E4 — foundational theory.

## E. Leadership structure and founder control

21. **Wang, D., Waldman, D. A., & Zhang, Z. (2014). Meta-analysis of shared leadership. *J. Applied Psychology* 99(2).** — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24188392/) [S]. *Supports:* shared leadership ρ≈.34 with team effectiveness; stronger on attitudes than hard performance (§I.5). *Strength:* E2 — 42 samples; correlational inputs.
22. **D'Innocenzo, L., Mathieu, J. E., & Kukenberger, M. R. (2016). *J. Management* 42(7).** — [Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0149206314525205) [S]. *Supports:* positive shared-leadership effect, 50 effect sizes; attenuates with task complexity (§I.5). *Strength:* E2. (Exact overall ρ not quoted — snippet-level confirmation only.)
23. **Nicolaides, V. C., et al. (2014). *Leadership Quarterly* 25(5).** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1048984314000691) [S]. *Supports:* shared leadership → performance via team confidence (§I.5). *Strength:* E2.
24. **Krause, R., Priem, R., & Love, L. (2015). "Who's in charge here? 25. **Feigen, M. A., Jenkins, M., & Warendh, A. (2022). 28. **Wasserman, N. (2017). 29. **Fahlenbrach, R. (2009). 31. **Ewens, M., & Marx, M. (2018). 32. **Wasserman, N.
