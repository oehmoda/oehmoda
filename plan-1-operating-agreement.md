## Recitals

1. The Company was organized under M.G.L. c. 156C on [DATE, ~one year ago] by five founders (the "Founders") with an informal understanding: an equal partnership, later modified to a per-founder reserved allocation plus contribution-based distribution of the remainder, never reduced to writing.
2. Founder contributions have since diverged; certain Founders are no longer active; certain employees have contributed more than certain Founders.
3. The Members now adopt this Agreement to (a) replace all prior oral understandings in their entirety, (b) allocate equity by objective, pre-agreed rules applied identically to Founders and employees, and (c) establish governance suitable for outside investment.

**Sources & rationale:** A written agreement replacing the oral one is required to obtain M.G.L. c. 156C §11 good-faith-reliance protection ([statute](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXII/Chapter156c/Section63)); formalizing before positions harden is urged by the renegotiation-failure findings of [Hellmann & Wasserman 2016](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2474) and the handshake-split case studies (Zipcar: [Ries 2012](http://www.startuplessonslearned.com/2012/04/founders-dilemmas-equity-splits.html); Snap/Brown $157.5M settlement: [TechCrunch 2017](https://techcrunch.com/2017/02/02/snapchat-reggie-brown/)).

## Article I — Formation and Purpose

1.1 **Entity.** [COMPANY NAME], LLC, a Massachusetts limited liability company under M.G.L. c. 156C. Principal office: [ADDRESS]. Registered agent: [NAME].
1.2 **Purpose.** Any lawful business, presently [BUSINESS DESCRIPTION].
1.3 **Term.** Perpetual until dissolved under Article XVII.
1.4 **Prior agreements superseded.** This Agreement supersedes all prior agreements and understandings, written or oral, concerning equity, governance, or compensation-in-equity.

## Article II — Definitions (key defined terms)

- **"Active Service"** — a calendar month in which a Service Provider devotes at least **[20] hours per week** on average to the Company, as certified quarterly by the Board under §9.3. Two consecutive months below threshold = "**Inactive**."
- **"Service Provider"** — any Founder or employee (or approved contractor) eligible to earn Units.
- **"Set-Aside Units"** — the Class F Units reserved for each Founder under Article IV (up to 5% of Base Units per Founder).
- **"Merit Pool"** — the pool of Class M Units allocated under Article V (75% of Base Units, plus forfeited Set-Aside).
- **"Slice"** — the unit of at-risk contribution defined in §5.2.
- **"Market-Rate Table"** — Schedule B, as adopted under §5.3.
- **"Freeze Event"** — the earlier of (a) closing of a bona fide priced equity financing from unaffiliated investors, or (b) two consecutive fiscal quarters of net profitability while all Service Providers are paid at least [90]% of Market-Rate Table compensation.
- **"Deemed Separation," "Good Leaver," "Bad Leaver"** — as defined in Article XI.
- **"Supermajority"** — [75]% of all outstanding voting Units.

## Article III — Units and Capital Structure

3.1 **Classes.** Authorized Units: **Class F** (Founder Set-Aside; voting), **Class M** (Merit Pool; voting), **Class P** (Profits Interests for employees/advisors; [voting/non-voting per counsel], issued with a threshold value per Rev. Proc. 93-27), and **Class O** (observer/advisor grants, non-voting, if used).
3.2 **Base allocation.** On the effective date: 25% of Base Units reserved as Class F (five tranches of 5%, one per Founder, unissued until earned); 75% as Class M, allocated per the Merit Ledger.
3.3 **Unit Ledger.** The Board maintains a Unit Ledger and quarterly Merit Ledger; both are available to all Members and Service Providers (information rights, §8.3).
3.4 **No new issuance** of any class except (a) as this Agreement provides, or (b) with Supermajority approval (§7.3).
3.5 **83(b)/threshold mechanics.** Each recipient of Units subject to vesting or profits interests shall make timely tax elections as counsel directs (83(b) within 30 days where applicable).

**Sources & rationale:** Profits-interest mechanics and tax treatment: [Carta](https://carta.com/learn/startups/compensation/equity-incentive-plans/profits-interest/), [Lyons Gaddis](https://www.lyonsgaddis.com/profits-interests-in-llcs-a-tax-friendly-alternative-to-stock-options/); K-1 partner status and W-2 incompatibility flagged for counsel ([Carta](https://carta.com/learn/startups/compensation/equity-incentive-plans/profits-interest/); phantom-unit alternative per [Startup Law Blog](https://www.thestartuplawblog.com/the-complete-guide-to-equity-compensation-for-startups/)).

## Article IV — Founder Set-Aside ("use it or lose it")

4.1 **Reservation.** 5% of Base Units is reserved for each Founder — a ceiling to be earned, not a grant.
4.2 **Earning schedule.** Each Founder's Set-Aside is earned in 48 equal monthly tranches, **retroactive to [FOUNDING DATE]**, but a tranche is earned **only for a month of Active Service**. A 12-month cliff applies: no tranche is earned unless the Founder has completed 12 cumulative months of Active Service. `[Decision point: the cliff may be credited for actual months served as a ratification concession — see implementation plan §6 Phase 1.]`
4.3 **Pause and resumption.** Inactivity pauses earning; a Founder who resumes Active Service resumes earning prospectively.
4.4 **Deemed Separation.** After [6] consecutive Inactive months, the Board shall give written notice; if Active Service does not resume within [60] days, the Founder is deemed separated as of the notice date.
4.5 **Forfeiture to the Merit Pool.** All unearned Set-Aside of a separated or deemed-separated Founder is **automatically transferred to the Merit Pool** — it is neither retained by the Founder, held in reserve, nor retired.
4.6 **No board seat, office, or salary by virtue of Founder status.** Founder status confers only the Set-Aside opportunity and Member rights.

**Sources & rationale:** Vesting as the commitment device replacing split renegotiation: [Wasserman 2012](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691158303/the-founders-dilemmas), [Seibel/YC](https://www.michaelseibel.com/blog/how-to-split-equity-among-founders); 4-yr/1-yr-cliff market standard: [Carta](https://carta.com/learn/equity/stock-options/vesting/), [Capbase](https://capbase.com/founder-vesting-schedules-best-practices/); forfeiture-to-pool keeps dead equity structurally near zero vs. the >10–15% diligence red flag ([Capbase](https://capbase.com/founder-vesting-schedules-best-practices/)); service-based accrual honors the use-it-or-lose-it intent while the objective Active Service test avoids litigating effort ([Breugst et al. 2015](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbvent/v30y2015i1p66-94.html) on procedural justice).

## Article V — Merit Pool (dynamic contribution equity)

5.1 **Principle.** Founders and employees earn from the Merit Pool **on identical terms**. Merit Pool percentages are provisional until the Freeze Event.
5.2 **Slices.** Each Service Provider accrues Slices equal to the fair market value of at-risk contributions × multiplier:
&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) **Time:** (Market-Rate salary − cash compensation actually paid) ÷ 2,000 × hours of Active Service × **2**;
&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) **Cash:** unreimbursed cash contributed or spent for the Company × **4**;
&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) **Property/IP/relationships:** Board-appraised FMV × **2**.
A Service Provider paid at or above Market Rate accrues no time Slices.
5.3 **Market-Rate Table.** Schedule B fixes a market salary per role (benchmarked mid-band for [GEOGRAPHY]) and evidence standards. Adopted at ratification; amended only by Board majority including the Independent Manager. Disputes over rates or claimed contributions are decided by the Independent Manager, finally and bindingly.
5.4 **Retroactive backfill.** The first Merit Ledger reconstructs all contributions from [FOUNDING DATE], self-certified with supporting evidence (calendars, commits, deliverables, bank records for cash) and Board-approved. Once approved, the backfill is final and not reopenable.
5.5 **Quarterly certification.** Within 30 days of quarter-end the Board certifies the Merit Ledger; each Service Provider's provisional Merit percentage = own Slices ÷ all Slices × Merit Pool size. The certified ledger is published to all Service Providers.
5.6 **Freeze.** Upon a Freeze Event, Merit percentages convert to fixed Class M Units. Frozen units are 50% earned at freeze; the remainder earns monthly over 24 months of continued Active Service. After freeze, new-hire equity comes from a new [10–15]% incentive pool created at the financing.
5.7 **Acceleration.** On a change of control followed within 12 months by termination without Cause or resignation for Good Reason, all unearned Set-Aside tranches and unearned frozen Merit units of the affected Service Provider accelerate in full (double trigger).

**Sources & rationale:** Slice formula, multipliers, at-market-pay rule, and freeze concept: [Moyer, *Slicing Pie*](https://slicingpie.com/the-magic-of-mutipliers/), [freeze guidance](https://support.thepieslicer.com/article/show/112619-how-do-i-freeze-bake-terminate-the-pie) — noting candidly that the model is practitioner-validated only (evidence review: [`research/academic-literature.md`](research/academic-literature.md) §3); automatic freeze answers the standard VC/legal objection to dynamic equity ([Silicon Hills Lawyer 2015](https://siliconhillslawyer.com/2015/06/01/founder-equity-grunt-funds/)); theory basis for contingent allocation under uncertainty: [Hellmann & Thiele](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1968696); transparency-as-fairness: [Breugst et al. 2015](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbvent/v30y2015i1p66-94.html); post-freeze re-vesting pre-empts standard Series A demands ([Capbase](https://capbase.com/founder-vesting-schedules-best-practices/)); double-trigger as investor standard: [Carta](https://carta.com/learn/equity/stock-options/vesting/); post-freeze pool size: [Carta 2025](https://carta.com/data/founder-ownership/), [Index Ventures](https://www.indexventures.com/rewarding-talent/).

## Article VI — Management: Board of Managers and Officers

6.1 **Manager-managed.** The Company is manager-managed under M.G.L. c. 156C. Management is vested in a **Board of Managers of three (3)**:
&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) **CEO seat** — held *ex officio* by the Chief Executive Officer; no term.
&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) **Founder seat** — one Founder in Active Service, elected annually by majority of voting Units; one-year renewable term; the seat lapses automatically if the holder becomes Inactive.
&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) **Independent seat** — a natural person who is not, and has not within [2] years been, a Founder, employee, contractor, or family member of any of them, and has no material economic relationship with any individual Member. Nominated by the other two Managers; first appointment requires unanimous Founder consent, thereafter Member-majority confirmation; [1–2]-year renewable term; removable by the vote that seated them.
6.2 **Initial CEO.** [NAME], designated by unanimous ratification of this Agreement. The CEO may be removed or replaced only by Supermajority (§7.3).
6.3 **Independent compensation.** [0.5]% as Class P profits interest, earning monthly over 24 months, no cliff, from the Merit Pool, terminating with the seat.
6.4 **Interim provision.** Until an Independent Manager is seated, the third seat is a second elected Founder seat (labeled as such), and any Deadlock is referred directly to mediation under Article XII.
6.5 **Officers and DRI Map.** The Board appoints officers. Schedule C (DRI Map) assigns each business function to exactly one owner. **Titles and functions are role assignments, not property**: the Board may reassign any role, including for sustained underperformance under Article IX, without affecting the holder's Units.
6.6 **Meetings.** The Board meets at least quarterly; the same meeting certifies the Merit Ledger and Active Service status and conducts the OKR review (§9.1). [Optional: one non-voting observer elected annually by employees.]
6.7 **Quorum and action.** Quorum: all three Managers (or two if a seat is vacant). Action: majority of the Board except where this Agreement requires more.

**Sources & rationale:** Small odd founder-controlled board pre-investment: [NYU](https://entrepreneur.nyu.edu/blog/2026/07/20/startup-board-structure/), [Rho](https://www.rho.co/blog/startup-board-composition), [Shockwave](https://shockwaveinnovations.com/early-stage-board-composition/); LLC board-of-managers construction: [UpCounsel](https://www.upcounsel.com/llc-board-of-managers); single-CEO principle: [Wasserman 2008 HBR](https://hbr.org/2008/02/the-founders-dilemma), [Wasserman 2017 SMJ](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/smj.2478) (correlational), [Horowitz](https://a16z.com/peacetime-ceo-wartime-ceo/), [Point Nine](https://medium.com/point-nine-news/can-a-startup-have-two-ceos-22eadd040d5e), [Gil](https://blog.eladgil.com/p/unequal-cofounders); DRI/role-overlap findings: [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/what-is-a-co-founder), [Pilot](https://pilot.com/tactical-guide/cofounders); titles-as-assignments and founder-transition resistance: [Wasserman 2012](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691158303/the-founders-dilemmas), [HSG](https://www.hsgcap.com/article/founder-to-ceo-transition/); independent compensation benchmarks: [FI FAST](https://fi.co/fast).

## Article VII — Decision Authority (tiered)

7.1 **CEO discretion (ordinary course).** All matters not reserved below, provided any single commitment over **$[25,000–50,000]** or outside the approved budget requires Board approval.
7.2 **Board majority.** Annual budget; officer appointment/removal below CEO; compensation changes; role reassignments; credit facilities within [limit]; settlement of claims under $[X]; Market-Rate Table amendments (with Independent concurrence, §5.3).
7.3 **Supermajority of Units ([75]%).** Admission of new Members; issuance of new Units or classes; incurrence of debt above $[X]; related-party transactions; removal/replacement of the CEO; removal of a Manager for Cause; sale, exclusive license, or disposition of material assets; distributions other than tax distributions; any transaction that would constitute a Freeze Event other than a bona fide financing.
7.4 **Unanimous consent of Members.** Only: (a) amendment of this Article VII or of any Member's economic rights in an adverse, non-pro-rata way; (b) dissolution outside Article XVII; (c) conversion or merger other than the pre-approved conversion in Article XVI.
7.5 **Construction.** The unanimity list shall be construed narrowly; nothing migrates into it by implication.

**Sources & rationale:** Tiered reserved-matters architecture: [ABA LLC Checklist](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/gpsolo/publications/gpsolo_ereport/2022/july-2022/llc-agreement-checklist/), [LegalGPS](https://www.legalgps.com/llc-operating-agreement/material-decisions); supermajority threshold norms (66.7–80%): [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/llc-supermajority-voting-for-amendments-how-it-works/); the keep-unanimity-short warning (every item is a per-Member veto) is standard practitioner guidance reflected in the same sources and in deadlock literature ([Jacobs Law](https://thejacobslaw.com/preventing-business-standstills/)).

## Article VIII — Members

8.1 **Voting.** Earned/issued voting Units vote; unearned Set-Aside and unaccrued Merit percentages do not.
8.2 **No employment rights.** Membership confers no right to employment, office, salary, or board seat.
8.3 **Information rights.** All Members and Service Providers receive quarterly financials, the certified Merit Ledger, and the Unit Ledger.
8.4 **Spousal consent.** Each married Member delivers a spousal consent to the transfer and repurchase provisions. `[Counsel: MA marital-property treatment.]`

## Article IX — Performance Management

9.1 **OKRs.** The Company adopts quarterly objectives; each officer and Founder in Active Service carries individual OKRs reviewed at the quarterly Board meeting before the full founder group.
9.2 **Reviews.** Annually: 360 peer review of each Founder-officer; Board review of the CEO (led by the Independent Manager).
9.3 **Active Service certification.** The Board certifies each Service Provider's Active Service status quarterly; certification is the trigger for §4.2 earning and §5.2 time Slices.
9.4 **Consequences.** Equity earning is a function of **time in Active Service only** — never of OKR attainment. Sustained underperformance (two consecutive missed review cycles) is grounds for role reassignment under §6.5, not equity forfeiture.

**Sources & rationale:** OKR/CFR structure and the decoupling of goals from compensation: [Doerr](https://www.betterworks.com/magazine/doerr-reveals-his-company-management-secrets-in-measure-what-matters); judging executives on organizational output: [Horowitz](https://a16z.com/peacetime-ceo-wartime-ceo/); metric-gated vesting rejected as gameable (design decision recorded in [implementation plan](implementation-plan.md) §5).

---

## Article X — Transfer Restrictions

10.1 **General prohibition.** No transfer, pledge, or encumbrance of Units except as this Article permits.
10.2 **ROFR.** Any proposed third-party transfer: first the Company (30 days), then the other Members pro rata (15 days), may purchase on identical terms.
10.3 **Tag-along.** If Members holding a majority of Units sell to a third party, every Member may join pro rata on identical terms.
10.4 **Drag-along.** A sale of the Company approved by Supermajority binds all Members on identical terms (customary limits: no personal liability beyond proceeds; pro-rata consideration).
10.5 **Permitted transfers.** Estate-planning transfers to a Member's trust remain subject to this Agreement.

**Sources & rationale:** ROFR/tag/drag mechanics: [Thomson Reuters Practical Law](https://legal.thomsonreuters.com/en/insights/articles/transfer-provisions-in-llc-agreements); drag-along as an investor prerequisite: same source; blanket-restriction-plus-exceptions structure: [ABA Checklist](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/gpsolo/publications/gpsolo_ereport/2022/july-2022/llc-agreement-checklist/).

---

## Article XI — Separation; Good Leaver / Bad Leaver

11.1 **Separation events.** Resignation; termination; death or disability; Deemed Separation (§4.4).
11.2 **Automatic on any separation.** Unearned Set-Aside forfeits to the Merit Pool (§4.5); unaccrued Merit participation ceases; accrued Slices/earned Units are retained subject to this Article.
11.3 **Good Leaver** (death, disability, termination without Cause, resignation for Good Reason, or Deemed Separation with full cooperation): retains earned Units, subject to ROFR/drag; Company holds a [12]-month option to repurchase earned Units at **fair market value** (independent appraisal; payable up to [3] years with interest at AFR).
11.4 **Bad Leaver** (termination for Cause — fraud, material breach, felony, willful abandonment — or breach of Article XIV): Company may repurchase **all** Units, earned included, at the **lower of cost or fair market value**.
11.5 **Cash-contribution floor.** Notwithstanding §11.4, a separated Service Provider is always entitled to recover documented cash contributions at face value (without multiplier) before any forfeiture is applied, payable when the Company reasonably can.
11.6 **Cause / Good Reason** defined conventionally per counsel.

**Sources & rationale:** Good/bad-leaver architecture and FMV-vs-cost pricing: [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/leaver-provisions-the-terms-that-founders-fear-the-most), [Ashfords](https://www.ashfords.co.uk/insights/blog/leaver-provisions-balancing-founder-fairness-and-company-protection) — with the US-practice caution that punitive clawbacks of earned equity invite enforceability challenges (reserve §11.4 for true Cause); cash-recovery-at-face-value floor: [Moyer's recovery framework](https://support.thepieslicer.com/article/show/112619-how-do-i-freeze-bake-terminate-the-pie).

## Article XII — Deadlock Resolution

12.1 **Deadlock** = (a) the Board fails on the same matter at two consecutive meetings, or (b) a Supermajority matter fails twice within 60 days with ≥[60]% but <[75]% support.
12.2 **Ladder.** (1) Mandatory principals' negotiation, 15 days; (2) mediation before a neutral (JAMS/AAA), 30 days; (3) for Board deadlocks, the Independent Manager's casting vote is final; (4) for Member deadlocks that threaten the business, any Member group holding ≥[40]% may initiate a **sealed-bid buy-sell (Texas shootout)**: each side submits a sealed per-Unit price; the higher bidder must buy the other side's Units at its own bid; closing within 90 days.
12.3 The buy-sell of §12.2(4) is unavailable before the second anniversary of this Agreement and during a pending Freeze Event.

**Sources & rationale:** Escalation-ladder design and precise deadlock definitions: [Jacobs Law (MA)](https://thejacobslaw.com/preventing-business-standstills/); shotgun/Texas-shootout mechanics and the cash-position bias (which the sealed-bid variant mitigates relative to a classic shotgun): [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/texas-shootout-clause-how-it-works-risks-and-tax-rules/); the cooling-off and unavailability windows follow the same practitioner guidance.

## Article XIII — Duties; Outside Activities

13.1 **Tailored duties.** To the extent permitted by M.G.L. c. 156C §63, fiduciary duties of Members and Managers are limited as expressly set out here; the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing is not waived.
13.2 **Outside activities.** Members not in Active Service may engage in outside ventures **not competitive** with the Company; Active Service Providers owe full business-time loyalty subject to Schedule C.
13.3 **Corporate opportunities.** Opportunities in the Company's current or planned lines of business belong to the Company; others are released.

**Sources & rationale:** §63 permits expanding or restricting duties by agreement ([statute](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXII/Chapter156c/Section63)); express tailoring matters because MA close-corporation duties under the *Donahue* line are otherwise strict ([Romano Law](https://www.romanolaw.com/can-fiduciary-obligations-be-waived-in-massachusetts/)). This is the article that lets inactive founders lawfully pursue other work while protecting the Company's core.

## Article XIV — IP, Confidentiality, Restrictive Covenants

14.1 **IP assignment.** Concurrently with signing, every Founder and Service Provider executes the Invention & IP Assignment (Schedule E) covering all past and future Company-related work product.
14.2 **Confidentiality.** Perpetual as to trade secrets; [5] years otherwise.
14.3 **Noncompetition.** Each Member holding ≥[5]% agrees, **as consideration for and tied to their equity ownership and any sale thereof** (not as a term of employment), not to compete in [FIELD] in [TERRITORY] for [18–24] months after ceasing to be a Member.
14.4 **Nonsolicitation.** [12–24] months, employees and customers.

**Sources & rationale:** Day-one IP assignment is the Snap/Brown lesson ([TechCrunch 2017](https://techcrunch.com/2017/02/02/snapchat-reggie-brown/)); structuring the noncompete as an owner/sale-of-business covenant exempts it from the MA Noncompetition Agreement Act's garden-leave and 12-month employment limits (G.L. c. 149 §24L; [Beck Reed Riden](https://beckreedriden.com/quick-guide-to-massachusetts-new-noncompete-law/)) — counsel must confirm the exemption's application to each Member.

## Article XV — Tax Matters

15.1 Partnership taxation; [CEO or CFO] as partnership representative.
15.2 **Tax distributions.** The Company distributes annually at least each Member's deemed tax liability on allocated income (at a fixed assumed rate) before any other distributions.
15.3 **Profits interests.** Class P Units issued with threshold values equal to Company FMV at grant; recipients file protective 83(b) elections; the Company applies Rev. Proc. 93-27/2001-43 safe harbors. `[Counsel: K-1 partner status for employee holders — evaluate phantom-unit alternative per implementation plan §3 if W-2 retention is preferred.]`

**Sources & rationale:** [Carta profits interests](https://carta.com/learn/startups/compensation/equity-incentive-plans/profits-interest/), [Lyons Gaddis](https://www.lyonsgaddis.com/profits-interests-in-llcs-a-tax-friendly-alternative-to-stock-options/), [Startup Law Blog](https://www.thestartuplawblog.com/the-complete-guide-to-equity-compensation-for-startups/).

## Article XVI — Pre-Approved Conversion

16.1 Upon a Freeze Event that is a priced financing, the Board is authorized — without further Member vote — to convert the Company into a **Delaware C-corporation**, mapping each Member's fixed post-freeze percentage to common stock, adopting NVCA-form documents, and creating the new incentive pool of §5.6.
16.2 Timing shall preserve QSBS eligibility from conversion (LLC interests cannot qualify; the §1202 5-year clock starts at conversion).

**Sources & rationale:** Institutional-investor expectation of Delaware C-corp + NVCA docs and QSBS mechanics: [Carta](https://carta.com/learn/startups/compensation/equity-incentive-plans/profits-interest/), [Startup Law Blog](https://www.thestartuplawblog.com/the-complete-guide-to-equity-compensation-for-startups/); pre-approving conversion removes a future unanimity choke-point, consistent with the keep-vetoes-short principle (Art. VII).

## Article XVII — Dissolution

By Supermajority (or unanimity if §7.4 applies); wind-up per c. 156C; liquidation waterfall: creditors → tax distributions accrued → Class P threshold mechanics → remaining pro rata by Units.

## Article XVIII — Amendment

By Supermajority, except matters listed in §7.4 (unanimous). No amendment may retroactively reduce earned Units or certified Slices.

## Article XIX — Miscellaneous

Governing law: Massachusetts. Disputes not resolved under Article XII: binding arbitration in [Boston] (JAMS/AAA), except equitable relief for Article XIV breaches. Notices, severability, counterparts, entire agreement per counsel's standard forms.
