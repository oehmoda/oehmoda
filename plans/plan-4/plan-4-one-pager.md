# Plan 4 — One-Pager: Governance, Equity, and Founder Inactivity

**For:** the five founders of [Company], LLC (Massachusetts) · **Prepared:** August 2026 · **Full detail:** `plan-4-design.md`

Plan 4 was drafted independently of the other plan branches.

---

## The three problems, and Plan 4's answer to each

**1. Governance.** The company moves to a manager-managed LLC with a three-seat Board of Managers: the CEO, one seat elected annually by the active founders, and one independent seat the founders choose together. One founder is appointed CEO by the Board and is removable by the Board — the removal mechanics are agreed now, before anyone knows whom they'll apply to. Each founder signs a one-page role charter with quarterly objectives. A short list of reserved matters (new equity, debt above a threshold, sale of the company, amending the operating agreement, related-party transactions) requires a two-thirds vote of all units. Conversion to a Delaware C-corp at the first qualified financing is pre-consented in the operating agreement, so no one can hold it hostage later.

**2. Equity.** Ten million units. Founders share **70%**, split once, now, by a structured retrospective assessment (each founder is scored by the other four on agreed dimensions — time committed, criticality of contribution, opportunity cost, and go-forward role — with the scores averaged and the split signed the same week). Every founder unit is subject to **four-year reverse vesting with a one-year cliff, backdated to each founder's actual start date**, so the past year of real work counts and roughly a quarter of each grant is already vested at signing. A **15% employee incentive pool** (profits interests) covers the ten employees at role-benchmarked grant sizes. The professor is offered **1.0%** on a two-year-vesting written advisory agreement that complies with the university's consulting and IP policies. The remaining **14%** stays unissued as a buffer, and all forfeited units return to it.

**3. Inactivity.** An "Active Founder" is one working at least 30 hours per week or on a Board-approved reduced schedule. A founder who falls below that bar gets written notice and 30 days to cure; after that, vesting pauses, and after six months of continued inactivity (or on resignation) the unvested units are forfeited to the buffer. **Vested units are never confiscated:** an inactive or departed founder keeps everything already earned, keeps the vote those units carry, and simply stops earning more and loses eligibility for the founder Board seat. There is no forced redemption of a good leaver — Massachusetts freeze-out law makes confiscation the most dangerous move available — but a for-cause departure triggers forfeiture of unvested units plus a company call on vested units at independently appraised fair value, with full process.

---

## Why this shape

- **One negotiation, then paper, then peace.** A live contribution ledger keeps the split permanently open and permanently arguable. Plan 4 spends one hard week settling the past, then lets standard time-based vesting govern the future — the mechanism investors already understand and won't ask you to unwind.
- **Backdated vesting is the fair credit for the unpaid year.** Nobody's first year is erased, and nobody gets free equity for the years they haven't served yet.
- **Inactivity is handled by *stopping the earn*, not by clawing back.** Forfeiture of unvested units is contractual and clean; confiscating vested units from a minority member is a textbook freeze-out claim. The design never needs to do it.
- **Small board, one CEO, pre-agreed exits.** Five founders cannot each hold a veto and also raise money. Two of five founder-controlled seats out of three is the market-standard compromise.
