# Governance & Equity for a 4–5 Founder Startup — One-Pager

*How modern successful startups govern and share equity: research-backed best practices, tailored to a 1-year-old, 5-founder Massachusetts LLC with ~10 employees. Inline citations link to primary sources; the full annotated bibliography is in [`references.md`](references.md) and the underlying research briefs in [`research/`](research/).*

## What the evidence says

- **People problems, not product problems, kill founding teams.** Wasserman's HBS dataset (~10,000 founders) attributes ~65% of high-potential startup failures to co-founder conflict ([Wasserman 2012](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691158303/the-founders-dilemmas); provenance caveat in [references.md](references.md) §A2); ~20% of YC startups lose a founder ([YC/Graham](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jodiecook/2025/01/06/the-18-common-mistakes-that-kill-startups-according-to-y-combinator/)). The #1 predictor of trouble is a "quick handshake" split made before contributions were knowable — 42% of teams decide in a day or less, 73% within the first month ([Hellmann & Wasserman 2016, *Management Science*](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2474)).
- **Static splits without vesting are the canonical mistake.** Roughly half of founding teams have no vesting or adjustment terms at all, and renegotiation later almost never works because the over-allocated founder holds a veto ([Hellmann & Wasserman 2016](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2474)). Zipcar ([Ries 2012](http://www.startuplessonslearned.com/2012/04/founders-dilemmas-equity-splits.html)), Facebook/Saverin, and Snapchat's $157.5M Reggie Brown settlement ([TechCrunch 2017](https://techcrunch.com/2017/02/02/snapchat-reggie-brown/)) are all failures of exactly this.
- **Perceived fairness — not equality — stabilizes teams** ([Breugst, Patzelt & Rathgeber 2015, *JBV*](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbvent/v30y2015i1p66-94.html)). Equal splits work when contributions stay comparable; when they diverge, what matters is a *process* everyone accepts as just.
- **Control concentrated in one CEO outperforms consensus.** Each level of founder control retained is associated with 17–22% lower pre-money valuations across 6,130 startups ([Wasserman 2017, *SMJ*](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/smj.2478) — correlational); investors reject co-CEO structures outright ([Point Nine](https://medium.com/point-nine-news/can-a-startup-have-two-ceos-22eadd040d5e); [Gil](https://blog.eladgil.com/p/unequal-cofounders); [Horowitz/a16z](https://a16z.com/peacetime-ceo-wartime-ceo/)).

## Governance: one CEO, small board, tiered decision rights

1. **Convert to a manager-managed LLC with a 3-person board of managers** (not 5): CEO + one other active founder + one independent the founders jointly pick. Odd number prevents deadlock; founders-only boards of 1–3 are the pre-seed norm ([NYU](https://entrepreneur.nyu.edu/blog/2026/07/20/startup-board-structure/); [Rho](https://www.rho.co/blog/startup-board-composition); LLC board mechanics per [UpCounsel](https://www.upcounsel.com/llc-board-of-managers)).
2. **Designate exactly one CEO** with day-to-day authority; other founders hold functional roles with real domain authority — checked by the board, not by co-deciding everything ([Wasserman 2008, HBR](https://hbr.org/2008/02/the-founders-dilemma); [Horowitz](https://a16z.com/peacetime-ceo-wartime-ceo/)).
3. **Tiered decision rights in the operating agreement** ([ABA LLC Checklist](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/gpsolo/publications/gpsolo_ereport/2022/july-2022/llc-agreement-checklist/); [LegalGPS](https://www.legalgps.com/llc-operating-agreement/material-decisions)): CEO discretion for ordinary course (spending threshold ~$25–50K escalates to board) → board majority for budgets, executive hires, litigation → member supermajority (e.g., 75% of units; typical range 66.7–80% per [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/llc-supermajority-voting-for-amendments-how-it-works/)) for new members, unit issuance, major debt, manager removal, asset sales → unanimity *only* for fundamental economic rights and dissolution. Keep the unanimous list short — every item on it is a veto.
4. **Deadlock ladder:** mandatory negotiation → mediation → independent's casting vote → buy-sell (shotgun/Texas-shootout) as last resort; define "deadlock" precisely ([Jacobs Law (MA)](https://thejacobslaw.com/preventing-business-standstills/); [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/texas-shootout-clause-how-it-works-risks-and-tax-rules/)).

## Founder roles

- **One owner per function (DRI map).** Most founder conflict starts as quiet role overlap; assign exactly one name to product, engineering, sales, ops, finance ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/what-is-a-co-founder); [Pilot](https://pilot.com/tactical-guide/cofounders)).
- **Titles are role assignments, not property.** Pre-commit in writing that the board can reassign roles for sustained underperformance; a replaced founder-executive can stay as member/board observer. Wasserman found 4 of 5 founders resist this transition — which is why pre-commitment matters ([Wasserman 2012](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691158303/the-founders-dilemmas); [HSG](https://www.hsgcap.com/article/founder-to-ceo-transition/)).

## Equity: two layers, everything vests

The existing informal deal (guaranteed founder base + contribution-based remainder) is a sound *dynamic* structure — it needs mechanics:

1. **Layer 1 — Founder base (fixed):** a guaranteed allocation per founder honoring the original partnership. Research favors generosity: motivation from fair treatment beats points saved ([Seibel/YC](https://www.michaelseibel.com/blog/how-to-split-equity-among-founders); [Breugst et al. 2015](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbvent/v30y2015i1p66-94.html)).
2. **Layer 2 — Contribution pool (dynamic):** the remainder allocated by a Slicing Pie–style formula until a freeze event: share = at-risk contributions ÷ everyone's, with unpaid time at fair-market rate ×2 and cash ×4, tallied quarterly ([Moyer, *Slicing Pie*](https://slicingpie.com/the-magic-of-mutipliers/)). The only widely used framework that objectively handles employees out-contributing founders — both earn from the same pool by the same formula. **Freeze the pie** at profitability/self-sufficiency or a priced round ([Moyer](https://support.thepieslicer.com/article/show/112619-how-do-i-freeze-bake-terminate-the-pie)). Caveats: no peer-reviewed validation exists, and VCs dislike un-frozen dynamic equity at financing ([Silicon Hills Lawyer 2015](https://siliconhillslawyer.com/2015/06/01/founder-equity-grunt-funds/)) — hence the automatic freeze.
3. **All units vest: 4-year monthly, 1-year cliff, retroactive credit** for time served — the industry-standard protection against inactivity, per YC and market practice ([Seibel](https://www.michaelseibel.com/blog/how-to-split-equity-among-founders); [Carta](https://carta.com/learn/equity/stock-options/vesting/); [Capbase](https://capbase.com/founder-vesting-schedules-best-practices/)). Add double-trigger acceleration on acquisition ([Carta](https://carta.com/learn/equity/stock-options/vesting/)).
4. **Inactive founders:** unvested units forfeited/repurchased at cost automatically; good-leaver/bad-leaver terms for the rest (bad leaver → buyback at cost; good leaver → keeps vested, company ROFR at fair value) ([Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/leaver-provisions-the-terms-that-founders-fear-the-most); [Ashfords](https://www.ashfords.co.uk/insights/blog/leaver-provisions-balancing-founder-fairness-and-company-protection)). Resolve current inactivity *now* — investors treat >10–15% "dead equity" as a diligence red flag ([Capbase](https://capbase.com/founder-vesting-schedules-best-practices/)).
5. **Employees:** grant **profits interests** (the LLC analog of options — tax-free at grant with a valuation hurdle under Rev. Proc. 93-27; [Carta](https://carta.com/learn/startups/compensation/equity-incentive-plans/profits-interest/); [Lyons Gaddis](https://www.lyonsgaddis.com/profits-interests-in-llcs-a-tax-friendly-alternative-to-stock-options/)) from a **10–15% pool** ([Carta 2025](https://carta.com/data/founder-ownership/); [Index Ventures](https://www.indexventures.com/rewarding-talent/)). Benchmarks: ~1.5–2% for a first key hire, <5% total across the first 10 ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/how-co-founders-split-equity/)).

## Performance measurement

Quarterly **founder OKRs** reviewed before the full founder group; annual **360 peer review** of each founder and board review of the CEO ([Doerr, *Measure What Matters*](https://www.betterworks.com/magazine/doerr-reveals-his-company-management-secrets-in-measure-what-matters)). Tie **vesting to time** — metric-gated vesting invites gaming (Doerr's decoupling principle) — but tie **role retention and the contribution pool** to measured performance; judge executives on their organization's output ([Horowitz](https://a16z.com/peacetime-ceo-wartime-ceo/)).

## Massachusetts-specific notes for the lawyer

Write the operating agreement (oral is legal under [M.G.L. c. 156C](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXII/Chapter156c/Section63) §2(9) but forfeits §11 good-faith-reliance protection); use **§63** to expressly tailor fiduciary duties — MA close-corporation duties under the *Donahue* line are otherwise strict ([Romano Law](https://www.romanolaw.com/can-fiduciary-obligations-be-waived-in-massachusetts/)); include ROFR + tag-along + drag-along transfer restrictions ([Thomson Reuters](https://legal.thomsonreuters.com/en/insights/articles/transfer-provisions-in-llc-agreements)); structure founder noncompetes as **tied to equity ownership/sale**, not employment — exempt from the 2018 MA Noncompete Act's garden-leave and 12-month limits ([Beck Reed Riden](https://beckreedriden.com/quick-guide-to-massachusetts-new-noncompete-law/)); and execute **IP assignments from all founders and employees immediately** (the Snapchat lesson, [TechCrunch 2017](https://techcrunch.com/2017/02/02/snapchat-reggie-brown/)).
