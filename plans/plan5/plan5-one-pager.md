# plan5 — One-Pager

**Client:** 1-year-old Massachusetts LLC · 5 founders (some now inactive) · ~10
employees · everyone unpaid · a Yale ML professor informally promised "a decent
share" at a premium rate. Full framework: `plan5-design.md`; counsel draft:
`plan5-operating-agreement.md`; sources with strength ratings: `plan5-references.md`.

## What the research found

1. **The most urgent problem isn't equity.** Ten unpaid employees at a for-profit
   MA company is an accruing violation of the Massachusetts Wage Act with
   **mandatory treble damages** (*Reuter v. Methuen*, 2022) and **personal
   liability for the founder-managers** (*Cook v. Patient Edu*, 2013). Equity
   can't substitute for wages (29 C.F.R. §531.27), "volunteers" don't exist at
   for-profits (FLSA/DOL), and the §148B ABC test blocks contractor relabeling.
   Fix within 30 days, before any equity work. [refs B1–B5]
2. **Renegotiation never happens on its own.** Longitudinal data: founder teams
   almost never voluntarily adjust stakes as contributions diverge (Hellmann et
   al. 2019) — exactly this team's story. Quick, equal splits (73% of teams split
   within a month) associate with lower valuations and less outside financing,
   ~10% of equity at stake (Hellmann & Wasserman 2017, selection-effect caveat
   honored). Conclusion: the split must be **self-executing by formula**, not by
   future votes. [A1, A3, A9]
3. **Perceived fairness of process, not equality of outcome, drives team
   survival** (Breugst 2015; Colquitt meta-analyses) — so rules published before
   outcomes, one public rate table, audit and appeal rights. [A2, A10]
4. **Leadership evidence supports a hybrid**: shared leadership adds performance
   *on top of* vertical leadership (3 meta-analyses, ρ≈.15–.35, correlational;
   they disagree on moderators and we say so) — but co-CEO/rotation lacks
   credible support (the peer-reviewed co-CEO result favors power gaps, i.e.,
   near-single leadership). Founder-CEOs are usually replaced after success
   events (50% out by year 3; 4 of 5 forced). Build turnover in, don't fight it.
   [A3–A5, A7]
5. **Investors punish dead equity and nonstandard structure at diligence**: >5%
   uninvolved holders flagged; SAFEs/NVCA docs assume Delaware C-corps; VC funds
   structurally avoid pass-throughs (UBTI/K-1s). Post-OBBBA QSBS (50/75/100% at
   3/4/5 yrs, $15M cap) rewards converting no later than the round. [B9, B13,
   C5]

## The design (fits the four fixed requirements)

- **Two channels only.** (1) **Founder Reserve, 25%**: each founder's 5% splits
  into 16 quarterly tranches of 0.3125%, each earned only by passing an objective
  Active-Service test that quarter (≥240 accepted hours or ≥50% of median
  full-timer points). Missed = forfeited into the pool, retroactively for Year 1
  too. (2) **Contribution Pool, 75% + recycled tranches**: a Slicing-Pie-style
  ledger — *identical formulas for founders, employees, and the professor*:
  unpaid hours × published market rate × 2; cash × 4; % = your points ÷ all
  points. Spreadsheet-computable; worked example in design §2.6.
- **Retroactive year**: same formula, seeded once from artifacts (commits, docs,
  calendars) with default 45/15 h-week fills, peer attestation, 120-day hard
  close.
- **Inactive founders**: keep exactly what they earned (worked example: 0.6–2.4%,
  not 5%), accrue nothing, buyable out at appraised FMV after 4 dead quarters —
  cap table cleaned before any round.
- **The professor**: same ledger as everyone; his "higher rate" honored only as
  *documented* consulting FMV (capped $450/h); total capped at 2.0% — above the
  full advisor-market range (FAST expert tier 0.8%; Carta medians 0.10–0.25%) yet
  bounded. Nothing accrues until Yale COI clearance and an IP-boundary covenant
  are signed; written agreement supersedes the oral promise.
- **Governance**: 3-manager board elected annually by all members (no founder
  seats); single accountable **CEO on renewable 12-month terms**, any member
  eligible, office decoupled from equity; DRIs own day-to-day via Amazon's
  one-way/two-way-door test; disagree-and-commit; 66⅔% supermajority for
  fundamental matters.
- **Disputes**: negotiation → mediation → binding expedited arbitration (Boston);
  documents-only fast track for ledger appeals; final-offer arbitration for
  valuations (our design choice — flagged, not market-standard); sealed-bid
  buyout as last resort with fairness backstop for counsel.
- **Investor path**: everything unconventional **sunsets at the Freeze Event**
  (financing ≥$2M, breakeven, or sale): ledger closes, percentages fix, LLC
  converts to Delaware C-corp (pre-consented), 12% option pool, NVCA forms,
  pre-agreed reverse vesting. Tax traps (profits-interest hurdles evaporating at
  conversion; 83(b) 30-day deadlines; QSBS timing) bracketed for counsel.

## Honesty box

No peer-reviewed outcome study of any dynamic-equity framework exists — we adopt
its arithmetic on indirect evidence and sunset it before investors arrive. The
leadership literature is correlational; we designed to be sound under either side
of its disagreements. Our research environment blocked direct URL fetches; every
source was corroborated via live web-search content instead, none from memory —
disclosure and per-source ratings in `plan5-references.md`. We never accessed any
other team's branch.
