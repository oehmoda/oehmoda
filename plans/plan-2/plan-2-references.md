**Evidence-strength scale**

- **E** — practitioner opinion, secondary summary, or anecdote — used only for color or where nothing stronger exists

## 1. Founder equity splits and their outcomes (academic)

1. **Hellmann, T. & Wasserman, N. (2017). "The First Deal: The Division of Founder Equity in New Ventures." *Management Science* 63(8), 2647–2666.** [Journal](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2474) · [open NBER PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w16922/w16922.pdf) · [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1805427) — **A (correlational; N = 1,476 founders / 511 US ventures).** Supports: ~32% of teams split exactly equally; equal splits associated with ≤1-day "quick handshake" negotiations; quick equal splitters raise less often and at lower valuations (~10% of equity / ~$450K NPV at stake); heterogeneity in idea, experience, and capital predicts unequal splits. Authors themselves flag the selection/signaling alternative explanation — we carry that caveat into the design.
2. **Breugst, N., Patzelt, H. & Rathgeber, P. (2015). "How should we divide the pie? Equity distribution and its impact on entrepreneurial teams." *JBV* 30(1), 66–94.** [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883902614000676) · [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbvent/v30y2015i1p66-94.html) — **A (qualitative longitudinal, N = 8 teams — small; treat as theory-generating).** Supports: *perceived justice* of the split relative to contributions, not equality per se, drives positive vs. negative team spirals. Basis for the design's transparency/process machinery.
3. **Hellmann, T., Schure, P., Tergiman, C. & Vo, D. (2019). "Ownership dynamics within founder teams." *SEJ* 13(3), 256–287.** [Wiley](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/sej.1328) · [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3020629) — **A (correlational, longitudinal admin data, BC Canada).** Supports: founder stakes are sticky; renegotiation happens mainly at financing events; fairness norms deter voluntary renegotiation. Basis for building the renegotiation mechanism into the contract now.
4. **Kotha, R. & George, G. (2012). "Friends, family, or fools." *JBV* 27(5), 525–543.** [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883902612000365) · [open PDF](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=4624&context=lkcsb_research) — **A (correlational, N = 611).** Supports: experienced founders tie equity to resource contribution and get more resources per point of equity granted.
5. **Hellmann, T. & Thiele, V. (2015). "Contracting Among Founders." *JLEO* 31(3), 629–661.** [Oxford](https://academic.oup.com/jleo/article-abstract/31/3/629/808939) · [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1968696) — **A (formal theory; no data).** Supports: vesting/milestone earn-in as the efficient contract for founder-team commitment problems — the closest thing to an academic foundation for dynamic equity.

## 2. Founding-team conflict, departure, and failure (academic + data)

6. **Gorman, M. & Sahlman, W. (1989). "What do venture capitalists do?" *JBV* 4(4), 231–248.** [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0883902689900141) — **A (survey, N = 49 VCs, 1984 — dated).** Supports: 95% of VCs cited ineffective senior management as a failure cause.
7. **Ensley, M., Pearson, A. & Amason, A. (2002). "Understanding the dynamics of new venture top management teams." *JBV* 17(4), 365–386.** [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883902600000653) — **A (correlational, 70 Inc. 500 ventures).** Supports: affective conflict harms growth; cognitive conflict helps; cohesion helps.
8. **Klotz, A. et al. (2014). "New venture teams: A review." *J. of Management* 40(1), 226–255.** [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0149206313493325) · [open PDF](https://www.hmieleski.com/Publications/Klotz_Hmieleski_Bradley_Busenitz_JOM_2014.pdf) — **A (systematic review).** Supports: team composition and emergent states drive venture outcomes; effects are contingent.
9. **Ruef, M., Aldrich, H. & Carter, N. (2003). "The structure of founding teams." *ASR* 68(2), 195–222.** [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/000312240306800202) — **A (representative PSED sample).** Supports: teams form by homophily/strong ties, not skill logic — context for why contribution-blind splits are common.
10. **Boeker, W. & Karichalil, R. (2002). "Entrepreneurial transitions: Factors influencing founder departure." *AMJ* 45(4), 818–826.** [AOM](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/3069314) — **A (correlational).** Supports: departure falls with founder ownership and board membership — equity as retention glue.
11. **Gregori, P. & Parastuty, Z. (2021). "Entrepreneurial team member exits." *Rev. Managerial Science* 15, 847–878.** [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11846-020-00377-1) — **A (systematic review, 41 articles).** Supports: exits are frequent and consequential; no single headline rate extractable.
12. **Carta co-founder departure data via SaaStr: "~65% of companies experience a co-founder departure before Series B."** [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/carta-co-founder-break-ups-are-accelerating) — **C-minus (platform data, but reported secondhand — flagged).**
13. **Wasserman, N. (2012). *The Founder's Dilemmas*. Princeton UP.** [Publisher](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691158303/the-founders-dilemmas) — **D (research-based book; CompStudy dataset ~10,000 founders).** Supports: rich-vs-king framing; static-split pathologies. The famous "65% of failures are people problems" figure is a *book-level descriptive claim with no peer-reviewed regression behind it* — cited only as such.
14. **Eisenhardt, K. & Schoonhoven, C. (1990). "Organizational growth… U.S. semiconductor ventures." *ASQ* 35(3), 504–529.** [Record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1446661cd44d1b75d90098453822e909395127a3) — **A (correlational, longitudinal).** Supports: larger, diverse, previously-acquainted founding teams → higher growth. **Conflicts with #15.**
15. **Greenberg, J. & Mollick, E. (2018). "Sole Survivors: Solo Ventures Versus Founding Teams."** [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3107898) · [NYU summary](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/experience-stern/faculty-research/venture-investors-take-note-solo-founders-outperform-teams) — **A-minus (working paper — peer-review status unverified; crowdfunded-venture sample).** Supports: solo founders survived better than teams in that population. Disagreement with #14 presented, not resolved, in design §12.3.

## 3. Governance, boards, CEO control (academic + practitioner)

16. **Wasserman, N. (2008). "The Founder's Dilemma." *HBR*, Feb. 2008.** [HBR](https://hbr.org/2008/02/the-founders-dilemma) · [reprint PDF](https://www.palominocap.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/5-HBR_Noam-Wasserman_The-Founders-Dilemma.pdf) — **D (practitioner article reporting research data; N = 212 startups).** Supports: 50% of founders out of CEO seat by year 3; <25% led IPO; 4 of 5 exits forced.
17. **Wasserman, N. (2017). "The throne vs. the kingdom: Founder control and value creation in startups." *SMJ* 38(2), 255–277.** [Wiley](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/smj.2478) · [open PDF](https://msbfile03.usc.edu/digitalmeasures/nwasserm/intellcont/Wasserman-2017-Strategic_Management_Journal-1.pdf) — **A (correlational, N = 6,130).** Supports: each added level of founder control (CEO + board) associated with 17.1–22.0% lower valuation. ## Cross-cutting honesty notes

1. **Causality is scarce.** Nothing in the founder-equity literature is experimental; the strongest designs are large-N longitudinal correlational studies (Hellmann & Wasserman; Wasserman 2017; Hellmann & Puri). Every "X is associated with Y" in the design means exactly that.
2. **The dynamic-equity mechanics (Slicing Pie multipliers, Demmler factors) have zero peer-reviewed outcome evidence.** They are adopted as transparent conventions, hardened by governance, not as validated science.
3. **Three specific figures rest on secondary reporting** and are flagged where used: the 65% co-founder-departure-before-Series-B stat (Carta via SaaStr), the FAST "v3 2026" revision (single secondary source; not relied on), and the Stanton/Raleigh case summaries (firm blogs; counsel should pull the opinions).
