# plan10 — One-Pager: Governance & Equity Design for a Five-Founder Massachusetts LLC

**The problem.** Five founders began as equal partners on a handshake; a year later some are inactive, some employees out-contribute some founders, ~15 people have worked unpaid, and a part-time Yale ML professor was orally promised "a decent share" at a premium rate. Nothing is written down. The company must now become **maximally attractive to future investors** — the fixed, overriding objective.

**What the research found (full citations and ratings in `plan-10-references.md`).**
- Quick handshake equal splits are the documented failure mode: 73% of teams split within a month; quick equal splitters raise at lower valuations (~$450K NPV at stake) — Hellmann & Wasserman 2017, *Mgmt Sci*; replicated directionally in 24k German startups. Lab evidence (Kagan et al. 2020, *Mgmt Sci*) shows the effect is **sorting**, and that **delaying the split until contribution is observable fixes it** — which is exactly this company's position today.
- What protects teams is **perceived justice and legible criteria**, not equality: unexplained pay dispersion corrodes interdependent teams (Shaw 2014); free-riding (g≈0.44 across 78 studies) disappears under identifiability (Karau & Williams 1993).
- Investors buy teams and clean control: management is the top VC decision factor (95%/47% — Gompers et al. 2020); each founder-control lever correlates with 17–22% lower valuations (Wasserman 2017); founder *replacement* causally improves performance (Ewens & Marx 2018, instrumented). Dead equity is the #1 cap-table red flag (Techstars/Carta), and ~23% of startups lose a co-founder within 3 years (Carta).
- Two legal landmines: the MA Wage Act (strict mandatory **treble damages**, personal founder liability, no equity substitute, no self-cure — *Reuter v. Methuen* 2022) and MA close-entity fiduciary law (*Donahue*, *Wilkes*, *Allison v. Eriksson* 2018) which makes any squeeze-play against inactive founders litigable and unfundable.

**The design (rationale in `plan-10-design.md`; terms for counsel in `plan-10-operating-agreement.md`).**
- **75% Contribution Pool — one formula, identical for everyone:** monthly CP = verified hours × published Boston-market rate *for the role actually performed* × 2 (non-cash); cash/property × 4. Evidence-linked logging, a full-transparency ledger, an elected 3-person verification committee with zero re-weighting discretion, 30-day challenge windows. Multipliers are practitioner convention (Slicing Pie) — labeled as such — and the model's documented critiques become design constraints: hard freeze before any priced round.
- **The unpaid year is settled once, through the same formula,** with evidence tiers (documented 100% / corroborated 85% / self-report 50%), ratified by 66⅔% of Units *plus* a majority of non-founder Units, granted now while FMV is low (profits interests where possible; appraisal + 83(b)s).
- **Founder Reserve (fixed at 5% × 5): genuinely earned** — 16 quarterly tranches over 4 years, each gated by a mechanical activity test (≥20 hr/wk verified, or certified deliverables); missed tranches forfeit **into everyone's pool**, not to other founders. Inactive founders keep exactly what the ledger says they earned, lose only the unearned reserve, and dilute naturally as others contribute — no confiscation, no confrontation, optional fair-value buyout.
- **The professor:** written advisor agreement supersedes the oral promise — 0.5–1.0% fixed grant (top of FAST/Carta advisor market, where the median pre-seed advisor gets 0.21%), 24-month vesting, **plus** ledger participation at his *documented* consulting rate (a premium honored as verifiable market data, the only form of dispersion the evidence says is safe). Conditioned on Yale COI clearance and IP provenance (Yale owns faculty inventions and litigates — *Fenn v. Yale*); no manager title (Yale one-day-per-week rule; Wage Act manager liability).
- **Governance:** manager-managed LLC; 3-seat annually elected Board (≥1 seat always non-founder); one CEO, appointed and removable by the Board; 3-tier decision matrix; the equity formula itself amendable only by 66⅔% + a majority of non-founder Units. One vote per Unit — expressly overriding the MA default that ties votes to capital contributed.
- **Investor path:** at the earlier of a ≥$1M priced round, a vote, or 36 months — freeze the ledger, convert by merger into a **Delaware C-corp** (LP/UBTI, SAFE/ISO mechanics, and QSBS make this a hard gate; YC requires it), install a 12–15% option pool and standard 4y/1y vesting, with all members pre-committed to cooperate. QSBS: post-2025 rules ($15M cap, 3/4/5-year tiers) start only at conversion — another reason not to linger.
- **Disputes:** negotiation → mediation → AAA arbitration in Boston; deadlock → appraised fair-value buyout; explicitly **no shotgun clause** (favors the richer party; a conflict signal to VCs; fiduciary-fraught in MA).
- **Gating item before any of it:** counsel-led Wage Act remediation — lawful payroll, bona fide member conversion, or pause; equity never substitutes for wages.

**Honest limits.** No peer-reviewed study validates dynamic equity end-to-end; the 2×/4× multipliers and evidence tiers are convention/construction, protected by supermajority entrenchment rather than science. Founder vesting is universal practice with only theoretical causal support. YC's near-equal-split advice conflicts with the academic split literature on the surface; the reconciliation (deliberate process + vesting) is what this design implements. Flagged-for-counsel items and every source's verification level are listed in `plan-10-references.md`.
