# Plan 4 — Full Design: Governance, Equity, and Founder Inactivity

**Company:** [Company], LLC, a Massachusetts limited liability company
**Founders:** five (the "Founders")
**Prepared:** August 2026
**Summary:** `plan-4-one-pager.md`

Plan 4 was drafted independently of the other plan branches. It is a business-terms
design for counsel to translate into an amended and restated operating agreement and
related documents; nothing here is legal or tax advice.

---

## 1. Design principles

1. **Settle the past once; govern the future with standard machinery.** The unpaid
   first year is credited through one structured retrospective negotiation and
   backdated vesting — not through a permanently open contribution ledger that must
   later be frozen and explained to investors.
2. **Nobody earns by title; everybody earns by time served in a role.** All founder
   equity reverse-vests. Stopping work stops the earn. Nothing already earned is
   ever confiscated.
3. **Decisions need an owner.** One CEO, a three-seat Board, and a short reserved-
   matters list replace five informal vetoes.
4. **Design for the departure you hope never happens.** Most founding teams lose a
   member before Series B. The exit terms are written now, symmetrically, before
   anyone knows which side of them they'll be on.
5. **Stay on the right side of Massachusetts law.** Closely held companies in
   Massachusetts owe members partner-like fiduciary duties; squeezing out a minority
   founder without process and fair value is the classic freeze-out claim. The
   design therefore never strips vested units from a good leaver, and any for-cause
   repurchase runs through independent appraisal and documented process. Separately,
   the ten unpaid employees are an urgent Wage Act problem that equity cannot fix —
   see §8.

## 2. Capitalization

Authorized: **10,000,000 units.**

| Block | Size | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| Founder units | **70%** (7,000,000) | Split by the §3 retrospective assessment; 4-year reverse vesting, 1-year cliff, backdated to each founder's start date (§4) |
| Employee incentive pool | **15%** (1,500,000) | Profits interests; role-benchmarked grants to the ten current employees and future hires; 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff |
| Advisor pool | **1%** (100,000) | Offered in full to the professor on a 24-month advisory agreement (§7) |
| Unissued buffer | **14%** (1,400,000) | Reserve for key hires and corrections; **all forfeited units return here**, not pro rata to holders |

The buffer absorbing forfeitures matters: it means one founder's departure benefits
the company's future hiring capacity rather than mechanically enriching the
remaining founders, which keeps departure decisions cleaner of conflict.

---

## 3. Splitting the founder block: one structured negotiation

The 70% founder block is divided **once**, within 30 days of adopting this plan, by
a structured retrospective assessment:

1. **Dimensions.** Each founder is scored on four agreed dimensions, equally
   weighted: (a) time committed to date, (b) criticality of contribution to date
   (product, sales, fundraising, team), (c) opportunity cost borne (salary forgone,
   cash contributed — cash counts double), and (d) go-forward role and expected
   contribution.
2. **Scoring.** Each founder scores the **other four** (never themselves) on each
   dimension, 1–10, with two sentences of written justification per score. Scores
   are collected blind by the company's counsel or an agreed neutral, then averaged.
3. **Conversion.** Each founder's share of the block = their total average score ÷
   the sum of all five founders' scores.
4. **Guardrails.** No founder receives less than **8%** or more than **35%** of the
   founder block absent unanimous consent; if raw scores breach a guardrail, the
   excess is redistributed pro rata among the others.
5. **Finality.** The resulting split is signed by all five in the operating
   agreement the same week. It is final and is not reopened by later events —
   later contribution differences are handled by vesting (§4), inactivity (§6),
   and future grants from the buffer, not by renegotiating the split.

Rationale: perceived fairness, not arithmetic equality, is what keeps founding
teams intact — and a split each founder helped score, justified in writing, is far
more durable than either a handshake equal split or a formula nobody negotiated.

## 4. Vesting

- **All founder units reverse-vest** over **48 months** with a **12-month cliff**,
  vesting monthly thereafter.
- **Backdating:** each founder's vesting commencement date is their documented
  actual start date, not the signing date. A founder who started 12 months ago
  signs with their cliff already met and ~25% vested — the fair, standard-form
  credit for the unpaid year.
- **Unvested units** carry no distribution rights and vote as directed by the Board
  until vested; vested units carry full economic and voting rights.
- **Acceleration:** double-trigger only (change of control + involuntary
  termination without cause within 12 months), 100% of unvested. No single-trigger
  acceleration.
- **Issue now, while value is near zero.** The retrospective issuance should close
  while fair market value is provably nominal, with a contemporaneous valuation and
  protective 83(b)-style filings, on counsel's and a tax advisor's sign-off (§8).

## 5. Governance

### 5.1 Board of Managers

The LLC converts from member-managed to **manager-managed** with a three-seat
Board:

| Seat | Held by | Selection |
|---|---|---|
| CEO seat | The then-serving CEO | Ex officio |
| Founder seat | An Active Founder other than the CEO | Elected annually by majority vote of Active Founders |
| Independent seat | A non-founder with relevant operating experience | Nominated by any founder, approved by 4-of-5 founder vote; 2-year term |

Board acts by majority. Quorum is all three seats (attendance by video counts). The
independent chair sets the agenda; the Board meets at least quarterly.

### 5.2 CEO

- The Board appoints **one** founder as CEO with a written mandate. No co-CEOs.
- The CEO is **removable by majority vote of the Board** (the CEO's own seat
  abstaining on that vote), for any reason, with the removal mechanics agreed now.
- A removed CEO remains a founder and member with all vested rights intact; removal
  from office is not "cause" for equity purposes unless §6.4 cause independently
  exists.

### 5.3 Roles and objectives

Each founder signs a **one-page role charter** (title, scope, decision rights,
reporting line) and agrees **quarterly objectives** with the CEO; the Board reviews
founder performance annually. Charters are amendable by the CEO with Board
approval — titles are not property.

### 5.4 Reserved matters — 66⅔% of all voting units

- Issuing new units or admitting members (other than from the pools per approved plans)
- Debt above $100,000 aggregate
- Sale, merger, or disposition of substantially all assets
- Amending the operating agreement
- Related-party transactions above $10,000
- Dissolution

Everything else is Board or CEO authority per the charters. Day-to-day operations
require no member vote.

### 5.5 Pre-agreed conversion and drag-along

The operating agreement contains **advance consent** to (a) conversion to a
Delaware C-corporation at or before the first equity financing of $1M+ on Board
approval, with units exchanging pro rata for common stock and vesting schedules
carrying over unchanged, and (b) a drag-along at 66⅔% for a Board-approved sale.
No member can later hold the financing hostage.

### 5.6 Deadlock

Member-level deadlock on a reserved matter, persisting 60 days: mandatory
negotiation (15 days) → mediation (30 days) → if still deadlocked, any member may
initiate a **fair-value appraised buyout** process in which the company (first) or
the other members (second) may purchase the initiating member's vested units at a
value set by an independent appraiser. No shotgun/Texas-roulette clauses — they
favor the richest founder, not the rightest one.

## 6. Founder inactivity

### 6.1 Definitions

- **Active Founder:** a founder providing at least **30 hours per week** of
  services, or working a reduced schedule approved in advance by the Board
  (parental leave, medical leave, and other statutory leaves are always
  Board-approved schedules and never trigger this section).
- **Inactive:** below the Active Founder bar for **60 consecutive days** without an
  approved schedule.

### 6.2 Process — notice, cure, pause, forfeit

1. **Notice.** The Board gives written notice of inactivity with specifics.
2. **Cure — 30 days.** Vesting continues during the cure period. Returning to
   Active status ends the process with no effect.
3. **Pause.** If not cured, **vesting pauses** (the schedule tolls; nothing is
   lost yet).
4. **Forfeiture.** After **6 further months** of inactivity, or immediately on
   voluntary resignation, all **unvested** units are forfeited to the buffer.
   Re-engagement before that point resumes the schedule where it paused, on Board
   approval.

### 6.3 What an inactive or departed founder keeps — always

- **Every vested unit.** No confiscation, no forced redemption of a good leaver.
- **The votes those units carry** on member matters.
- Information rights required by the LLC Act and the operating agreement.

What they lose: further vesting, eligibility for the founder Board seat, any
officer role (at Board discretion), and their role charter. This "stop the earn,
keep the earned" structure is deliberately freeze-out-proof: the company never
takes anything a founder has already earned, so the classic Massachusetts
minority-oppression claim has nothing to attach to.

### 6.4 Cause — the one exception

**Cause** (defined tightly: fraud, embezzlement, felony conviction, willful and
material breach of the operating agreement or IP assignment, uncured after notice)
triggers: forfeiture of all unvested units **and** a company call option on vested
units at **fair value set by an independent appraiser**, exercisable within 180
days, with written findings by the disinterested Board members. Fair value plus
documented process is what distinguishes a defensible for-cause repurchase from a
freeze-out; counsel drives this procedure whenever it is invoked.

### 6.5 Transfers and liquidity

- Units are non-transferable except to estate-planning vehicles, with a company
  **right of first refusal** on any permitted third-party offer.
- The company **may** offer (but never compel) a repurchase of a departed founder's
  vested units at appraised fair value; any such buyout is mutual-consent only.

## 7. The professor

- The oral promise is replaced with a **written advisory agreement**: **1.0%**
  (the full advisor pool), vesting monthly over **24 months**, no cliff, terminable
  by either side on 30 days' notice (vesting stops at termination).
- Scope: advice and introductions only — no supervision of the company by
  university students or use of university facilities, and an express
  representation that the engagement complies with the university's consulting-time
  limits, conflict-of-interest disclosure requirements, and IP policy.
- All IP the professor creates for the company is assigned to the company, with a
  carve-out schedule for anything the university's patent policy already claims —
  identified now, in writing, not discovered in diligence.

## 8. Immediate legal workstream (counsel, this month — before or with signing)

1. **Wage Act remediation.** Ten employees (and any founder treated as an
   employee) working unpaid at a for-profit violates the Massachusetts Wage Act
   and minimum wage law: damages are trebled and mandatory, managing founders are
   personally liable, late payment does not cure, and deferral-for-equity
   agreements are void. Counsel to size the exposure, get everyone onto lawful
   payroll immediately, and structure remediation of the arrears. Equity grants
   are in addition to, never instead of, wages.
2. **Retrospective issuance mechanics.** Contemporaneous valuation supporting
   near-zero fair market value; profits-interest structuring for the employee
   pool; protective 83(b)-style filings for the founder issuance; note that any
   employee receiving units changes payroll-tax status — coordinate with the
   payroll fix above.
3. **IP and assignment sweep.** Every founder, employee, and the professor signs
   IP assignment and confidentiality agreements covering all work to date.
4. **Sign everything the same week:** amended and restated operating agreement
   (split, vesting, Board, reserved matters, inactivity, deadlock, conversion
   consent), role charters, advisory agreement. An unsigned plan is a list of
   things the founders once agreed about.

## 9. What Plan 4 deliberately does not do

- **No live contribution ledger.** Continuous point-tracking keeps the split
  permanently contestable, adds bookkeeping the team won't sustain, and must be
  frozen and explained at financing. Plan 4 buys the same fairness with one
  retrospective negotiation plus backdated vesting, then uses machinery investors
  already price.
- **No guaranteed per-founder floor going forward.** Title guarantees nothing;
  only vesting time in an active role earns.
- **No forced redemption of good leavers.** The most legally dangerous and
  trust-destroying tool in the Massachusetts toolbox; the design never needs it.
- **No five-person board and no co-CEOs.** Five vetoes is how this company got a
  year of unpaid limbo; the fix is fewer seats, one accountable chief executive,
  and a short reserved-matters list protecting what genuinely needs protecting.
