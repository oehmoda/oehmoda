# Plan-3 One-Pager — What the Evidence Says, and What We Built on It

*Citations in brackets (e.g., [F1]) map to `plan-3-references.md`, which carries URLs and honest evidence-strength ratings for every source. Full design: `plan-3-design.md`. Draft operating agreement: `plan-3-operating-agreement.md`.*

## Best practices found in the research

**1. The split process matters more than the split numbers.** Quick, un-negotiated "handshake" equal splits correlate with lower first-financing valuations (~10% of firm equity, ≈$450K NPV at stake; 1,476 founders / 511 ventures) [F1]; non-negotiated equal splitters are ~3× more likely to regret their split [F4]; *perceived justice* of the split, not equality, drives team spirals [F5]. But the literature genuinely disagrees on equal splits themselves: YC recommends near-equal defaults [F10], the market has moved toward 50/50 (45.9% of two-founder teams in 2024, up from 31.5% in 2015) [C12], while a 24,194-startup German study finds unequal splitters perform ~24.5% better [F9]. Practical upshot (and the authors' own reading of [F1]): run a deliberate, criteria-based process — which is what a contribution-based system is.

**2. Founder departure is a base-rate event, so vesting is non-negotiable.** ~23% of co-founders are gone by year 3 and ~40% by year 8 (22,352 founders on Carta) [C11]; ~50% of founders are out of the CEO seat by year 3, usually involuntarily [F3][F4]. Standard machinery: 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff, 1/48 monthly, reverse-vesting repurchase at cost, double-trigger acceleration [F10][F11]. Theory says vesting is precisely the fix for team-formation under skill uncertainty [F7].

**3. Dead equity is a diligence red flag.** Average dead equity on 733 tech cap tables tripled 2008–2011 to >$1.5M [C10]; VC folklore flags concern at ≥10% dead [C13]. Best practice: use-it-or-lose-it earning, automatic forfeiture, pre-agreed buybacks — never negotiate with a departed founder after the fact [C13][C14].

**4. Dynamic (contribution-based) equity works mechanically but has zero peer-reviewed validation and must freeze before financing.** Slicing Pie prices every at-risk contribution at fair market value with multipliers (non-cash ×2, cash ×4) and divides the pie by accumulated slices [C1]; critics (and even advocates' own bridging tools) agree the pie must convert to a fixed cap table at a funding event because VCs require frozen, conventional ownership [C3][C4]. We adopt the mechanics, cap them with a hard Freeze Event, and disclose the evidence gap [C4].

**5. Value the work, not the title — and price seniority through market rates.** Slicing Pie prices coding, management, and vision identically *in kind*: market salary for that role ÷ 2,000 hrs, ×2 risk multiplier — a deliberate rejection of the observed 10–15 pp "idea premium" [C1][C15]. This one rule satisfies "identical terms for founders and employees" while still paying a famous ML professor more per hour than a junior engineer, because his *market rate* is higher, not his rules ($2,500–$5,000/day is the professor-advisor norm) [D11].

**6. Employee and advisor benchmarks.** Early-hire medians (Carta, ~50k startups): hire #1 1.5%, #2 0.85%, #3 0.5%, #4 0.44%, #5 0.33% [D1][D2]; median seed option pool 12.5% [D5]. Advisors: FAST matrix 0.15–1.0% (Expert tier at startup stage: 0.8%) with 2-year monthly vesting [D8], but *measured* medians are far lower (pre-seed 0.21%, seed 0.12%; only ~10% of pre-seed advisors get ≥1%) [D9]; ~1% is defensible only for marquee names [D10][D11].

**7. Boards: small, odd, with an independent tie-breaker; one CEO.** Norms: 3 seats at seed (2 founders + 1 investor), 5 at Series A (2–2–1 with independent swing) [G12]; off-board founders get observer rights [G14]. Founder-control maximalism correlates with 17–22% lower valuations per control level (6,130 startups) [F2]. Co-CEOs: the peer-reviewed study finds equal shared command is the worst configuration [G3]; the pro-co-CEO HBR data is survivor-biased [G4]. Decision rule with the best evidence: "consensus with qualification" — seek consensus, CEO decides when it fails [G5]; but avoid dominant-CEO autocracy in turbulent environments [G7]. Five-founder teams are actually good news for growth (larger founding teams outperform [G9]) *if* conflict is governed [G6].

**8. Deadlock: ladder, not shotgun.** Escalation → mandatory mediation (30–60 days) → binding/final-offer arbitration; independent-director casting vote; deadlock-triggered put/call — anything beats judicial dissolution [G15][G16]. Shotgun clauses are fair only between symmetrically informed, symmetrically funded parties — lab-tested; cash-poor founders (ours) are exploitable [G10].

**9. LLC equity = profits interests now; Delaware C-corp before institutional money.** Profits interests are the only tax-free-at-grant LLC instrument (Rev. Procs. 93-27/2001-43): hurdle ≥ current FMV, protective 83(b) within 30 days, recipient becomes a K-1 partner and stops being W-2 [A1][A2][A4][A9]. VCs require Delaware C-corps (fund-LP UBTI/ECI issues; SAFEs assume C-corp; >90% of Carta companies are corporations, ~88% Delaware) [B4][B5]. QSBS (C-corp only) improved sharply for stock issued after July 4, 2025: $15M cap, $75M asset ceiling, 50/75/100% exclusion at 3/4/5 years — and the clock starts at conversion, so don't convert late [B1][B2].

**10. Massachusetts specifics are the sharpest constraints.**
- **Wage Act (existential):** ~10 unpaid employees = accruing strict-liability treble-damage claims ($15/hr minimum floor), personally against managing founders; deferral agreements and equity promises are void; volunteers are illegal at for-profits; "contractor" labels fail the ABC test's prong B [E8]–[E14]. The only compliant paths: pay on schedule, or make workers bona fide member-partners.
- **LLC law:** duties can be expanded/restricted but (unlike Delaware) probably not eliminated [E1][E7]; Donahue-line "utmost good faith" applies among close-LLC members, including to freeze-outs via job termination [E4]–[E6]; members can always resign on 6 months' notice and there is NO statutory expulsion — the operating agreement must build it [E2].
- **Noncompetes:** mostly unavailable (garden-leave consideration; unenforceable against non-exempt employees — which unpaid workers are); use nonsolicits + NDAs + forfeiture conditions, all outside the Act [E15][E16].
- **Academic advisor:** Yale's one-day-in-seven consulting cap [D14]; Yale owns inventions in his research scope (license, don't assign) [D15]; COI disclosure/Provost approval [D13]; no students without written university-cleared agreements [D17]; publication review 30–60 days, never a veto [D21].

## What we built on this evidence (summary)

100% of pre-financing fully diluted units: **25%** founder set-asides (5 × 5%, earned quarterly over 4 years, use-it-or-lose-it, forfeits into the contribution pool), **62%** Contribution Pool allocated by a published points formula — *identical for founders and employees*: points = hours × (market salary for the work performed ÷ 2,000) × 2, cash ×4 — applied retroactively to Year 1 from git/calendar evidence and prospectively via monthly logs, frozen at the earlier of a $1M+ financing, C-corp conversion, or 36 months; **1%** advisor grant to the professor (FAST Expert-tier + marquee premium, 24-month vesting) with the right to earn more in the pool at his market rate under everyone's rules; **12%** future-hire pool. Governance: manager-managed LLC, 5-seat board (CEO + 3 member-elected + 1 independent tie-breaker), single CEO with annual 360 review and majority-removal, consensus-with-qualification decision rule, escalation→mediation→final-offer-arbitration deadlock ladder, no shotgun. Entity: MA LLC now (profits interests + protective 83(b)s), pre-authorized Delaware C-corp conversion at first qualified financing. Inactive founders: tranches lapse automatically, roles follow election not the "founder" title, earned units keep their vote. **Urgent counsel flag: Massachusetts Wage Act remediation for the ~10 unpaid employees precedes everything else.**
