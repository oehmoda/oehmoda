## Best practices from the research (each claim sourced and rated in the references file)

**1. Unwritten, equal-ish splits are the classic failure pattern — and you are living it.** About one-third of founding teams split equally, usually after ≤1 day of discussion; those "quick handshake" teams raise outside capital less often and at lower valuations, with the value at stake estimated near 10% of the equity ([Hellmann & Wasserman, *Management Science* 2017](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2474)). What predicts healthy teams is not equality but *perceived justice* — a split members see as proportional to contribution ([Breugst, Patzelt & Rathgeber, *JBV* 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883902614000676)). Teams almost never renegotiate on their own; stakes typically move only when investors force it ([Hellmann et al., *SEJ* 2019](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/sej.1328)). Fixing this now, on paper, is the single highest-value governance act available to you.

**2. People problems, not product problems, kill companies.** 95% of surveyed VCs cited ineffective senior management as a failure cause ([Gorman & Sahlman, *JBV* 1989](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0883902689900141)); relationship conflict in founding teams damages growth while task conflict helps ([Ensley et al., *JBV* 2002](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883902600000653)); and roughly 65% of startups experience a co-founder departure before Series B (Carta data via [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/carta-co-founder-break-ups-are-accelerating)). A 5-founder team should *plan* for departures, not hope against them.

**3. Everything vests; nothing is granted for showing up.** The 4-year/1-year-cliff norm is near-universal (≥95% of cliffs on Carta sit at 12 months — [Carta](https://carta.com/learn/equity/stock-options/vesting/); [Holloway](https://www.holloway.com/g/equity-compensation/sections/vesting-and-cliffs)); economic theory shows milestone/time earn-in is the efficient fix for founder-team commitment problems ([Hellmann & Thiele, *JLEO* 2015](https://academic.oup.com/jleo/article-abstract/31/3/629/808939)). "Dead equity" above ~10% of the cap table is an explicit investor red flag ([ISA Ventures](https://isaventures.substack.com/p/dead-equity-on-your-cap-table-is); [Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/founder-basics-founders-stock/)).

**4. Contribution-based ("dynamic") equity exists and works, but must freeze before investors arrive.** The leading practitioner frameworks value time at fair-market rates with risk multipliers (Slicing Pie: 2× unpaid time, 4× cash — [slicingpie.com](https://slicingpie.com/the-grunt-fund-calculator/)); critics converge on one fix: the dynamic ledger must convert to a conventional cap table at the first financing ([Sentient Law](https://www.sentientlaw.com/dynamic-equity-slicing-pie/); [Equity Matrix](https://equitymatrix.io/blog/slicing-pie-problems)). No peer-reviewed evaluation of these tools exists — the academic support is indirect (vesting theory + the justice findings). We say so plainly.

**5. Benchmarks: employees, advisors, pools.** Median first-10-hire grants run 1.49% (hire #1) down to ~0.3% (hire #5+) ([Carta 2024 data](https://community.carta.com/c/corporations-updates/equity-for-your-first-8-hires)); option pools 10–20% ([Index Ventures](https://www.indexventures.com/rewarding-talent/esop-size); [Holloway](https://www.holloway.com/g/equity-compensation/sections/the-option-pool)). Advisor medians are small — 0.21% at pre-seed, with only ~10% of advisors at ≥1% ([Carta](https://carta.com/learn/startups/equity-management/advisory-shares/)); the FAST framework tops out at 1.0% for an expert-level advisor at the earliest stage, on 2-year vesting ([fi.co/fast](https://fi.co/fast)). A part-time academic advisor above ~1–1.5% would be far outside market.

**6. LLC mechanics are a minefield; the exit ramp is a Delaware C-corp.** Profits interests are tax-free at grant only prospectively ([Rev. Proc. 93-27](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/irs-guidance/revenue-procedures/irs-addresses-treatment-partnership-profits-interest-services/dl8p)/[2001-43](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-01-43.pdf)); equity for the *past* unpaid year is taxable compensation at FMV — so it must be issued **now, while FMV is provably near zero** ([The Tax Adviser 2025](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2025/mar/the-complex-simplicity-of-partnership-interests-exchanged-for-services/)). Any employee receiving units stops being W-2 ([Rev. Rul. 69-184](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/Rev_Rul_69-184.pdf)). VCs require C-corps (UBTI, preferred stock, ISOs — [Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/choosing-correct-business-entity-basics/)), and converting sooner starts the QSBS clock (post-OBBBA: up to $15M excluded, 50/75/100% at 3/4/5 years — [Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.com/insights/changes-to-section-1202-qualified-small-business)).

**7. Massachusetts law gives with one hand and takes with the other.** The LLC Act allows almost any operating-agreement design, including tailored duties and forced-redemption triggers ([c. 156C §63](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXII/Chapter156C/Section63), [§28](https://law.justia.com/codes/massachusetts/part-i/title-xxii/chapter-156c/section-28/)) — but SJC caselaw imposes partner-like "utmost good faith and loyalty" among members of closely held companies, and squeezing out an inactive founder without process and fair value is the textbook freeze-out claim ([Donahue 1975](https://law.justia.com/cases/massachusetts/supreme-court/1975/367-mass-578-2.html); [Wilkes 1976](https://law.justia.com/cases/massachusetts/supreme-court/1976/370-mass-842-2.html); applied to LLCs in [Pointer 2009](http://masscases.com/cases/sjc/455/455mass537.html) and [Allison 2018](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/spr-jud-crt-mas-suf/1896955.html)). **And the ten unpaid employees are a live emergency:** unpaid work at a for-profit violates the Wage Act and the $15 minimum wage, with *mandatory treble damages*, personal liability for managing founders ([Cook v. Patient Edu 2013](https://massachusettssupremecourtopinions.justia.com/2013/06/14/cook-v-patient-edu-llc)), no retroactive cure ([Reuter 2022](https://law.justia.com/cases/massachusetts/supreme-court/2022/sjc-13121.html)), and no valid waiver — deferral-for-equity agreements are void "special contracts" ([§148](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section148); Stanton, per [Gordon LLP](https://www.gordonllp.com/blog/agreements-to-defer-salary-violate-the-wage-act/)).

**8. The professor needs a contract, not a promise.** Yale caps consulting at one day in seven, bans involving Yale students/personnel, requires COI disclosure, and claims IP under its Patent Policy — and litigates ([Fenn v. Yale, 2003](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/283/615/2576942/)). A vague oral promise of "a decent share" is likely unenforceable for indefiniteness yet still supports a quantum-meruit claim ([case law commentary](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/ensuring-definiteness-in-oral-agreements-and-viability-of-quantum-meruit-claims-under-california-law/view)) — the worst of both worlds until papered.

**9. Small boards, one CEO, pre-agreed removal.** Market norm is a 3-person seed board with at most two founder seats even for 4–5 founder teams ([Rho](https://www.rho.co/blog/startup-board-composition); [YC's Series A template](https://www.ycombinator.com/series_a_term_sheet) is 2+1); smaller decision bodies correlate with higher firm value ([Yermack, *JFE* 1996](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304405X95008445)). Founder control itself carries a measured price — 17–22% lower valuation per added level of control ([Wasserman, *SMJ* 2017](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/smj.2478), N = 6,130). Half of founder-CEOs are replaced by year three, four times out of five involuntarily ([Wasserman, HBR 2008](https://hbr.org/2008/02/the-founders-dilemma)) — so removal mechanics must be agreed before anyone knows whom they'll apply to. Practitioner consensus favors a single accountable CEO; the mildly positive co-CEO evidence comes from a tiny, self-selected public-company sample ([Pilot](https://pilot.com/tactical-guide/cofounders); [HBR 2022](https://hbr.org/2022/07/is-it-time-to-consider-co-ceos)).

## The framework we designed from this evidence (details in `plan-2-design.md`)

- **Cap table (10M units):** 25% founder set-asides (5 × 5%, earned quarterly, use-it-or-lose-it, forfeitures flow back to the pool) · 65% Contribution Pool allocated by a points ledger on identical terms for founders and employees · 2% advisor pool (professor offered 1.0% on a 24-month advisory agreement) · 8% unissued buffer.
- **The ledger:** Points = verified hours × published market rate for the *function performed* (coding/management/vision each priced off a Board-adopted Rate Card) × 2.0 risk multiplier while unpaid × 0.8–1.2 quality factor; cash counts at 4×. Year 1 is settled once, retroactively, by an evidence-based audit on the same formulas. Shares of the pool = your points ÷ all points; the ledger freezes at the first financing.
- **Governance:** 3-seat Board (CEO + elected Active-Founder seat + independent chair), single CEO selected and removable by the Board, written founder roles and quarterly objectives, supermajority (66⅔%) member vote for reserved matters, pre-consented Delaware C-corp conversion at financing.
- **Inactive founders:** keep every unit they *earned*; earn nothing further; no reserved seats; no confiscation (freeze-out-proof by design).
- **Disputes:** ledger disputes go to fast, binding baseball-style expert determination; member deadlock goes negotiation → mediation → appraised-value buyout; no shotgun clauses.
- **Immediate legal to-dos (counsel):** Wage Act remediation plan for all 15 people; contemporaneous valuation + protective 83(b) filings for the retroactive issuance; professor's advisor agreement with Yale-policy representations.
