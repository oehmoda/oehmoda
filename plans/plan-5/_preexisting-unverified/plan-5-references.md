## A. Equity division — academic

1. **Hellmann, T. & Wasserman, N. (2017), "The First Deal: The Division of Founder Equity in New Ventures," *Management Science* 63(8).** [Journal](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2474) · [NBER w16922](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16922) · [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1805427)
   *Supports:* ~⅓ of 511 ventures (1,476 founders) split equally; equal and especially *quick* equal splits associate with lower first-round pre-money valuations; ~10% of firm equity (~$450K NPV) at stake; heterogeneity in idea generation, prior founding experience, and capital contribution predicts unequal splits. Used to justify: contribution-differentiated split; treating negotiation/measurement effort as value-adding; delivering idea premiums through bounded mechanisms.
   *Strength:* **Tier 1.** Correlational with selection concerns the authors themselves model (the effect concentrates in "quick" splitters); does not show that *changing* a split changes outcomes.

2. **Breugst, N., Patzelt, H. & Rathgeber, P. (2015), "How should we divide the pie? Equity distribution and its impact on entrepreneurial teams," *Journal of Business Venturing* 30(1).** [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883902614000676)
   *Supports:* perceived (in)justice of the split drives positive/negative team-interaction spirals with imprinting effects on outcomes. Used to justify: procedural transparency (published ledger, written Kicker justifications, appeals), no clawback of earned equity, and treating process quality as a first-order design goal.
   *Strength:* **Tier 2.** Eight teams over six months; rich but small-N qualitative; direction of causality plausible but not established.

3. **Wasserman, N. (2008), "The Founder's Dilemma," *Harvard Business Review*.** [HBR](https://hbr.org/2008/02/the-founders-dilemma) · **Wasserman (2006), "Rich versus King," Academy of Management Best Paper Proceedings.** [PDF](https://www.noamwasserman.com/nwasserman/Rich_vs_King-Proceedings_with_abstract.pdf) · [MIT seminar version](https://web.mit.edu/iandeseminar/Papers/Fall2006/Wasserman.pdf)
   *Supports:* founders retaining both CEO seat and board control hold stakes ~52% as valuable; wealth–control tradeoff. Used to justify: merit-selected removable CEO, elected board, no founder-status seats (requirement #3).
   *Strength:* **Tier 1/2 boundary.** Large proprietary samples (460–6,000+ ventures) but HBR write-up; correlational — control-keeping founders may differ systematically.

## B. Leadership & organization — academic

4. **Wang, D., Waldman, D.A. & Zhang, Z. (2014), "A meta-analysis of shared leadership and team effectiveness," *J. Applied Psychology* 99(2).** [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24188392/) · [APA](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2013-38206-001)
   *Supports:* shared leadership ↔ team effectiveness, ρ ≈ .34 (42 samples); stronger for attitudes/processes than hard performance. Used to justify: preserving shared-leadership *behavior* via the Decision Rights Matrix.
   *Strength:* **Tier 1 (meta-analytic, correlational).** Mostly non-startup teams; outcome measures heterogeneous.

5. **D'Innocenzo, L., Mathieu, J.E. & Kukenberger, M.R. (2016), "A meta-analysis of different forms of shared leadership–team performance relations," *J. Management* (via *Leadership Quarterly* companion literature).** [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1048984314000691)
   *Supports:* corroborates the positive shared-leadership relation with measurement moderators. *Strength:* **Tier 1**, same caveats as #4.

6. **Ensley, M.D., Hmieleski, K.M. & Pearce, C.L. (2006), "The importance of vertical and shared leadership within new venture top management teams," *Leadership Quarterly* 17(3).** [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1048984306000051) · [author PDF](https://www.hmieleski.com/Publications/Ensley_Hmieleski_Pearce_LQ_2006.pdf)
   *Supports:* in startup TMTs (66 Inc.-500 + 154 random firms), both vertical AND shared leadership predict revenue/employee growth — shared adds to, does not replace, a clear vertical leader. This is the single most on-point academic result for our "one CEO + delegated shared leadership" synthesis.
   *Strength:* **Tier 1.** Cross-sectional survey; common-method concerns; startup-specific (a rarity).

7. **Lee, S. (2022), "The myth of the flat start-up: Reconsidering the organizational structure of start-ups," *Strategic Management Journal* 43(1): 58-92.** [Wiley](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/smj.3333) · [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3835538) · [Wharton summary](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/podcast/knowledge-at-wharton-podcast/why-startups-need-hierarchical-structure/)
   *Supports:* flat hierarchy → more novelty but "haphazard execution and commercial failure"; startups need managerial structure earlier than founders think. Used against leaderless/rotating options.
   *Strength:* **Tier 1.** Large sample (game-development startups); one industry; correlational with instrumental attempts.

8. **Feigen, M.A., Jenkins, M. & Warendh, A. (2022), "Is It Time to Consider Co-CEOs?," *Harvard Business Review*.** [HBR](https://hbr.org/2022/07/is-it-time-to-consider-co-ceos)
   *Supports (weakly):* 87 public co-CEO firms returned 9.5%/yr vs 6.9% single-CEO average, 1996–2020. We cite it to *reject* building on it: tiny N, survivorship bias, large public firms.
   *Strength:* **Tier 3.** Non-refereed; selection-biased sample; presented honestly as such.

9. **Ewens, M. & Malenko, N. (2025), "Board Dynamics over the Startup Life Cycle," *Journal of Finance* (NBER w27769).** [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27769) · [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3640898) · [data](https://github.com/michaelewens/vc_backed_boards)
   *Supports:* startup boards begin entrepreneur-controlled; independents join ~second financing and serve a mediation role in VC-founder conflict. Used to justify: 3-seat elected board now, pre-architected expansion to 5 with an independent.
   *Strength:* **Tier 1.** Comprehensive panel data; descriptive/correlational.

## C. Equity mechanics — industry & practitioner

10. **Carta — vesting norms.** [Vesting explained](https://carta.com/learn/equity/stock-options/vesting/) · [Founder shares](https://carta.com/learn/startups/equity-management/founder-shares/) · [Founder Ownership Report 2026](https://carta.com/data/founder-ownership-2026/)
    *Supports:* 4-year/1-year-cliff as dominant standard; ≥95% of cliffs are 1 year; investor expectation of founder vesting; re-vesting at financing is common. *Strength:* **Tier 3** — very large proprietary cap-table dataset, non-refereed.

11. **Carta — option pools.** [Option pool sizing](https://carta.com/learn/startups/equity-management/option-pool/)
    *Supports:* median seed option pool ≈ 12.5%; 10–15% U.S. norm. Used for the post-Crystallization plan. *Strength:* **Tier 3.**

12. **Moyer, M., *Slicing Pie Handbook* (free sample).** [PDF](https://slicingpie.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Slicing-Pie-Handbook-FREE-SAMPLE.pdf)
    *Supports:* the dynamic-equity ledger concept; 2× non-cash and 4× cash risk multipliers; terminating the dynamic model at breakeven/first financing. *Strength:* **Tier 4** — pure practitioner convention; no outcome studies exist. Our multipliers adopt his convention for legibility, not because the specific values are evidence-based.

13. **Equity Matrix — Slicing Pie critiques.** [Problems](https://equitymatrix.io/blog/slicing-pie-problems) · [Guide](https://equitymatrix.io/blog/slicing-pie-guide)
    *Supports:* documented gaming vectors (multiplier optimization), missing cliff/performance expectations, fixed-cap-table conversion pain, missing legal wrapper — each answered by a specific design feature (capital-call-only 4×, Activity Gate, Crystallization, this OA draft). *Strength:* **Tier 4** (vendor commentary; itself sells a competing product — read with that bias in mind).

14. **Dead equity.** [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/blog/dealing-dead-equity) · [ISA Ventures](https://isaventures.substack.com/p/dead-equity-on-your-cap-table-is) · [Vestd](https://www.vestd.com/blog/dead-equity-what-it-is-why-it-matters-and-how-to-avoid-it?hs_amp=true)
    *Supports:* VCs treat ≥10% non-contributor equity as a red flag; cures are vesting, buybacks, transfer restrictions. *Strength:* **Tier 3/4** — law-firm and investor commentary; the "10%" threshold is folk-quantitative, not studied.

15. **Founder Institute — FAST agreement.** [FAST](https://fi.co/fast) · [Overview](https://fi.co/insight/the-founder-institute-s-standard-advisor-agreement-for-startups-fast)
    *Supports:* advisor benchmarks 0.15–1.0% by stage/engagement; 2-year vest, 3-month cliff. Basis for the professor's Track A sizing. *Strength:* **Tier 3** — widely adopted standard; not outcome-validated.

16. **BLS Occupational Employment & Wage Statistics.** [Software developers OOH/OEWS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/software-developers.htm) · [May 2025 national table](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ocwage.t01.htm)
    *Supports:* median software-developer wage $133,080 (May 2024) → $64/hr Rate Table anchor. *Strength:* **Primary government statistics** — the strongest data cited anywhere in this plan; note it is a national median, and Boston-metro OEWS figures should be substituted at the first annual re-benchmark.

## Where the literature disagrees — stated, not papered over

- **Shared vs. vertical leadership:** the meta-analytic literature (##4-6) says shared leadership helps; the structure literature (#7) says flatness hurts. These are reconcilable (behavior ≠ structure) and our design depends on that reconciliation — but note the reconciliation is *our synthesis*, not itself a tested proposition.
- **Equal splits:** Hellmann & Wasserman (#1) show equal splits correlate with worse financing outcomes, but they do not show unequal splits *cause* better ones; Breugst et al. (#2) imply an *unjust-feeling* unequal split can be worse than an equal one. Our answer — differentiated but procedurally transparent — is a bet on both findings being about perceived legitimacy.
- **Dynamic equity:** practitioner enthusiasm (#12) vs. practitioner criticism (#13), with zero peer-reviewed outcome evidence on either side. Lowest-confidence major component of this design; mitigated by the Crystallization sunset.
- **Co-CEOs:** the only quantitative support (#8) is too weak to act on; we say so rather than citing it as support for shared structures.
