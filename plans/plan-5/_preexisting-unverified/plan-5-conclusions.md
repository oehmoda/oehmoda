## Top-line conclusions

1. **The most urgent problem is legal, not organizational.** Ten unpaid employees put the company in ongoing violation of the Massachusetts Wage Act: strict liability, automatic treble damages, personal liability for managing founders, and equity-for-wages deferral agreements are unenforceable. Cure first (convert contributors to bona fide members, or pay minimum wage — counsel's call), before any equity mechanics.

2. **Sequencing:** cure wages → run a one-time Retrospective Reckoning of Year-1 contributions while paper value is near zero (minimizes ordinary-income tax on retroactive equity) → convert promptly to a Delaware C-corporation (investor standard; starts the post-OBBBA $15M QSBS holding clock, which only begins at conversion).

3. **Governance (two-line summary).** One CEO with real day-to-day authority, but on an annual mandate — appointed and removable at any time by a 3-seat board that is fully elected by all equity holders (no seats reserved for founders), with domain leads owning decisions in their areas via a written decision-rights matrix. Big-ticket items (amendments, new equity, M&A, rate-table changes) need a 66⅔% supermajority of units, and disputes escalate negotiation → mediation → arbitration, with a sealed-bid auction (deliberately not a shotgun clause) as the last-resort deadlock breaker.

4. **Where the CEO comes from.** An open internal selection: the board runs a structured candidacy process that any member — founder or employee — may enter, then appoints by board majority. Founder status confers no advantage. The mandate renews annually by board vote; members holding ≥40% of units can force a confidence review. Rationale: merit-selected, replaceable leadership avoids the "dead founder in charge" failure mode and the finding that control-clinging founders hold stakes worth roughly half as much (Wasserman).

5. **Evidence synthesis behind that structure.** Shared leadership as *behavior* helps teams (Wang et al. 2014 meta-analysis, ρ≈.34; Ensley et al. 2006 in startups specifically), but flat *structure* damages commercial execution (Lee 2022, SMJ), and co-CEO evidence is too thin and survivorship-biased to build on (Feigen et al. 2022, n=87 public firms). Hence: single accountable CEO in structure, shared leadership preserved at the operating level through delegated decision rights.

6. **Equity (adds to 100%).**
   - **25% Founder Reserved Pool:** 5% per founder, earned in 16 quarterly tranches of 0.3125%, each gated on verified activity (≥390 hours/quarter or ≥75% of median top-contributor points). A missed quarter's tranche cancels permanently into the Contribution Pool — the required "use it or lose it."
   - **74% Contribution Pool:** identical formula for founders and employees — verified hours × BLS-anchored work-category rate (e.g., $64/hr software engineering, $80/hr senior/management/vision work) × 2.0 unpaid-risk multiplier (4.0 for board-approved cash), with a bounded ±25% peer-reviewed impact kicker. Applied retroactively to Year 1 via evidence-backed reconstruction (documented hours 100%, attested 75%, contested 50%), then quarterly until it freezes at the first priced round, after which the company switches to salaries plus a market-standard option pool (~12.5%).
   - **1% Advisor Pool** for the professor and future advisors.

7. **Inactive founders:** keep every unit actually earned (no clawbacks of earned equity — fairness-perception research shows unjust splits poison teams); forfeit every unearned tranche automatically; face a company call option at fair market value on departure; and, like everyone, pre-commit to up to 25% investor-required re-vesting at the first priced round.

8. **The professor:** no status-based rate. Either (a) a standard advisor grant of 0.5% (up to 0.8% with a ≥10 hrs/month commitment) on FAST benchmarks, or (b) participation in the Contribution Pool at a rate set by the same benchmark petition open to everyone, hard-capped at 2× the base engineering rate. Any grant is conditioned on a written release of the informal "decent share" promise (a live promissory-estoppel risk), Yale conflict-of-interest clearance (one-day-a-week limit, equity review), and representations that no Yale resources or Yale-claimable IP are involved.

9. **Honest evidence limits.** All leadership and equity-split findings are correlational; dynamic-equity ledgers have zero peer-reviewed outcome evidence (confidence rests on the procedural-justice literature plus documented practitioner experience); the Slicing Pie 2×/4× multipliers are convention, not data. The design prefers reversible choices (annual mandates, elections, a ledger that sunsets at first financing) precisely because of these limits.
