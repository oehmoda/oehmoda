## 1. Diagnosis: what the evidence says about this exact situation

The company's current state is a compound of four well-documented failure patterns:

**(a) The quick-handshake equal split, never written down.** About one-third of founding teams split equity exactly equally, and equal splits are strongly associated with "quick negotiations" of a day or less. Quick equal splitters go on to raise outside financing less often and at lower pre-money valuations, with the value at stake estimated at roughly 10% of the equity, ~25% of an average founder's stake, or ~$450K in NPV ([Hellmann & Wasserman, *Management Science* 63(8), 2017](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2474); open-access [NBER w16922](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w16922/w16922.pdf); N = 1,476 founders in 511 US tech and life-science ventures; correlational — the authors themselves caution equal splitting may partly *signal* weaker teams rather than cause bad outcomes). This team started as "equal partners" with nothing in writing — the modal case in that data.

**(b) Contribution divergence with no renegotiation mechanism.** Founder stakes are sticky: teams rarely renegotiate on their own because fairness norms make reopening the split feel like an attack, and in practice relative stakes change mainly when external financings force it ([Hellmann, Schure, Tergiman & Vo, *Strategic Entrepreneurship Journal* 13(3), 2019](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/sej.1328); longitudinal administrative data, correlational). Meanwhile, what actually keeps teams functional is *perceived justice* — members' sense that ownership tracks contribution. In a six-month longitudinal study of eight venture teams, splits perceived as just triggered positive interaction spirals; splits perceived as unjust triggered conflict, disengagement, and exit intentions ([Breugst, Patzelt & Rathgeber, *Journal of Business Venturing* 30(1), 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883902614000676); qualitative, N = 8 teams — small sample, rated accordingly in the bibliography). Here, some founders have gone largely inactive while some employees out-contribute some founders: on the Breugst evidence, the current de facto arrangement is a perceived-injustice engine.

**(c) Team conflict and departures as the dominant startup killer.** In the classic VC survey, 95% of venture capitalists cited ineffective senior management as a cause of portfolio failure, versus 43% citing market factors ([Gorman & Sahlman, *JBV* 4(4), 1989](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0883902689900141); survey, N = 49 VCs, 1984 — dated but foundational). Relationship (affective) conflict in top teams is negatively associated with new-venture growth while task (cognitive) conflict is positively associated ([Ensley, Pearson & Amason, *JBV* 17(4), 2002](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883902600000653); N = 70 Inc. 500 ventures). Departures are the norm, not the exception: roughly 65% of startups experience a co-founder departure before Series B (Carta cap-table data reported via [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/carta-co-founder-break-ups-are-accelerating) — secondary report of platform data). The oft-quoted claim that "65% of high-potential startup failures are caused by people problems" traces to Wasserman's book ([*The Founder's Dilemmas*, Princeton UP 2012](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691158303/the-founders-dilemmas)) and has **no peer-reviewed regression behind it** — we cite it only as a book-level descriptive claim.

**(d) Live legal exposure.** Ten unpaid employees at a Massachusetts for-profit is an ongoing Wage Act violation accruing mandatory treble damages with personal liability for managing founders (§10.1 below) — independent of anything this framework does about equity. And the professor's unwritten "decent share" is simultaneously (i) probably too indefinite to enforce as a contract and (ii) a viable quantum-meruit claim against the company (§7).

**Design conclusion.** The founders' two fixed requirements — capped, earn-it-or-lose-it founder set-asides, plus a single objective contribution system on identical terms for founders and employees — are strongly *directionally* supported by this evidence: formal theory shows contingent, earn-in equity mitigates exactly this team-commitment problem ([Hellmann & Thiele, "Contracting Among Founders," *JLEO* 31(3), 2015](https://academic.oup.com/jleo/article-abstract/31/3/629/808939); theory model), and experienced founders allocate equity selectively against contribution rather than by relationship ([Kotha & George, *JBV* 27(5), 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883902612000365); N = 611). What the founders' requirements leave open — and what this document decides — is the machinery: governance, formulas, retroactivity, the professor, taxes, and enforcement.

## 2. Design principles derived from the evidence

1. **Perceived justice through visible process, not just numbers.** The system must let every participant recompute every allocation ([Breugst et al. 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883902614000676)). Hence: published Rate Card, artifact-backed logs, a committee founders don't control, and appeal rights.
2. **Pay for the function, not the title.** All five founders are engineers whose work spans coding, management, and vision; the labor market already prices those functions separately. Valuing hours at the market rate *of the function performed* is the only objective way to compare them (mechanism borrowed from Slicing Pie's fair-market-rate valuation — [slicingpie.com](https://slicingpie.com/the-grunt-fund-calculator/) — a practitioner framework with **no peer-reviewed validation**, which we adopt for its mechanics, not its evidence base).
3. **Everything prospective vests; everything retrospective is audited.** Vesting/earn-in is the theoretically efficient contract ([Hellmann & Thiele 2015](https://academic.oup.com/jleo/article-abstract/31/3/629/808939)) and the universal market norm (≥95% of grant cliffs sit at 12 months — [Carta](https://carta.com/learn/equity/stock-options/vesting/); [Holloway](https://www.holloway.com/g/equity-compensation/sections/vesting-and-cliffs)).
4. **Minimize dead equity by construction.** Dead equity above ~10% of the cap table is an explicit investor red flag ([ISA Ventures](https://isaventures.substack.com/p/dead-equity-on-your-cap-table-is); practitioner opinion), and departing founders are its main source ([Jolis/TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/10/17/solve-the-dead-equity-problem-with-a-longer-founder-vesting-schedule/)). Use-it-or-lose-it set-asides and earned-in-arrears contribution units mean nobody ever holds equity they didn't work for.
5. **Small decision bodies, one accountable leader.** Larger boards correlate with lower firm value in the canonical study ([Yermack, *JFE* 40, 1996](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304405X95008445); public-company data), decision effectiveness degrades ~10% per member beyond seven ([Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/effective-decision-making-and-the-rule-of-7/); consulting research, not peer-reviewed), and startup norms put at most two founders on a seed board even for 4–5 founder companies ([Rho](https://www.rho.co/blog/startup-board-composition); [Feld et al., *Startup Boards* 2d ed.](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Startup+Boards:+A+Field+Guide+to+Building+and+Leading+an+Effective+Board+of+Directors,+2nd+Edition-p-9781119859284)).
6. **Design for founder-CEO turnover in advance.** Half of founder-CEOs are out of the seat by year three, under a quarter lead their IPO, and in four of five cases the departure is forced ([Wasserman, *HBR* Feb. 2008](https://hbr.org/2008/02/the-founders-dilemma), N = 212 startups; corroborated by [Hellmann & Puri, *J. Finance* 57(1), 2002](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1540-6261.00419)). Removal mechanics agreed while nobody is being removed are cheap; the same mechanics negotiated mid-crisis are ruinous.
7. **Freeze the dynamic system before investors arrive.** Every serious critique of dynamic equity converges on investor-facing complexity ([Sentient Law](https://www.sentientlaw.com/dynamic-equity-slicing-pie/); [Equity Matrix](https://equitymatrix.io/blog/slicing-pie-problems); practitioner critiques). The ledger therefore has a hard freeze at the first qualified financing, converting to a boring, conventional cap table.
8. **Never confiscate earned equity.** Massachusetts SJC caselaw imposes "utmost good faith and loyalty" among members of closely held companies and treats squeeze-outs as fiduciary breaches ([Donahue v. Rodd Electrotype, 367 Mass. 578 (1975)](https://law.justia.com/cases/massachusetts/supreme-court/1975/367-mass-578-2.html); [Wilkes v. Springside, 370 Mass. 842 (1976)](https://law.justia.com/cases/massachusetts/supreme-court/1976/370-mass-842-2.html); applied to LLCs in [Pointer v. Castellani, 455 Mass. 537 (2009)](http://masscases.com/cases/sjc/455/455mass537.html) and [Allison v. Eriksson, 479 Mass. 626 (2018)](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/spr-jud-crt-mas-suf/1896955.html)). Inactive founders keep what they earned; they simply stop earning.

## 3. Entity and tax strategy (fixed requirement 3: maximum investability)

### 3.1 The three constraints that decide it

1. **Retroactive equity is taxable at FMV, now or never-cheaply.** Any interest that transfers current value for past services is §83 ordinary compensation income at fair market value at grant ([The Tax Adviser, Mar. 2025](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2025/mar/the-complex-simplicity-of-partnership-interests-exchanged-for-services/); [Kirkland & Ellis memo](https://www.kirkland.com/-/media/publications/article/2002/03/compensating-the-service-partner-with-partnership/compensating-the-service-partner.pdf?rev=831ccf76fdea4cc1a67366e523aebe8c)). A true profits interest under [Rev. Proc. 93-27](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/irs-guidance/revenue-procedures/irs-addresses-treatment-partnership-profits-interest-services/dl8p)/[Rev. Proc. 2001-43](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-01-43.pdf) is tax-free at grant precisely because it carries **zero** day-one value — so by definition it cannot deliver the past year's compensation. The only cheap moment to settle Year 1 is **immediately, while the company's FMV is demonstrably nominal** (pre-revenue, no paid staff, no financing), supported by a contemporaneous valuation and protective 83(b) elections (30-day hard deadline; standardized [IRS Form 15620](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f15620.pdf), now e-fileable per [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/alerts-practices-erisa-online-filing-of-section-83b-elections)).
2. **Units make employees partners.** Anyone holding an LLC interest stops being a W-2 employee and becomes a K-1 partner with self-employment tax, quarterly estimates, and loss of employee-benefit eligibility ([Rev. Rul. 69-184](https://bradfordtaxinstitute.com/Endnotes/Rev_Rul_69-184.pdf); [The Tax Adviser 2013](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2013/nov/brock-nov2013/); [Foley](https://www.foley.com/p/102m475/thinking-about-granting-profits-interests-to-your-employees-four-reasons-you-may/)). For ten rank-and-file employees this is a heavy, morale-burning administrative tax.
3. **VCs will require a Delaware C-corp anyway** — pass-through K-1s and UBTI are unacceptable to funds' LP base, and preferred stock, standardized NVCA documents, and ISOs exist only in corporate form ([Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/choosing-correct-business-entity-basics/); [Kruze](https://kruzeconsulting.com/blog/why-do-vcs-like-to-invest-in-delaware-c-corps/)).

### 3.2 Recommendation: settle in the LLC, then convert early — target within 2 quarters

**Step 1 (days 0–90):** Adopt the operating agreement; run the Year-1 Retrospective Contribution Audit (§5.6); obtain a third-party valuation evidencing nominal FMV; issue the audited Year-1 units and file protective 83(b)s within 30 days of each issuance.
**Step 2 (days 90–180):** Convert to a **Delaware C-corporation**. Massachusetts' LLC Act has merger provisions ([G.L. c. 156C §§59–63](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXII/Chapter156C/Section60)) but no native LLC-to-corporation conversion, so counsel will typically merge the MA LLC into a new Delaware corporation or use a Delaware-side statutory conversion ([DGCL §265 mechanics, ABA Business Law Today](https://businesslawtoday.org/2020/04/conversion-domestication-transfer-continuation-entities-dgcl/)); the incorporation is generally tax-free under §351 via one of the [Rev. Rul. 84-111](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2007/apr/incorporatingapartnershiporllcdoesrevrul84-111needupdating/) forms, with traps (liabilities over basis, unvested interests without 83(b)s) flagged for counsel ([Baker Tax Law](https://mbakertaxlaw.com/llc-conversions/)). Post-conversion, everyone holds common stock; employees return to (newly lawful, paid — §10.1) W-2 status; future grants are restricted stock/options; the ledger keeps running in stock until the freeze.

**The QSBS trade-off, stated honestly.** Converting *later* raises the §1202 per-issuer cap, because basis for the 10×-basis prong is asset FMV at conversion ([Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2906/2023-08-22-considering-converting-llc-corporation-here-are-qsbs); [Hanson Bridgett](https://www.hansonbridgett.com/publication/240301-7000-llcs-may-help-maximize-qsbs-benefits) — the "$3M at conversion supports $30M exclusion" play). Converting *sooner* starts the holding-period clock and puts all future appreciation inside the exclusion ([Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/maximizing-qsbs-for-entrepreneurs/)). Post-OBBBA (stock acquired after July 4, 2025), the cap is the greater of **$15M or 10× basis**, with tiered exclusion of **50%/75%/100% at 3/4/5 years** and a $75M asset ceiling ([Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.com/insights/changes-to-section-1202-qualified-small-business); [McLane Middleton](https://www.mclane.com/insights/obbba-changes-to-the-qsbs-regime-under-section-1202-a-comprehensive-overview/)). For this company — current FMV ≈ nominal, so the 10×-basis prong is worthless today and the flat $15M floor applies either way — **early conversion dominates**: there is almost no pre-conversion appreciation to sacrifice, the clock starts sooner, and the employee-as-partner problem (§3.1.2) ends. `[⚖ counsel to confirm against the founders' exit expectations]`

### 3.3 Interim instruments (the ≤2 quarters of LLC life)

- Founders and any employee who affirmatively opts in: real Class C/Class F units (partner status accepted, tax distributions per OA Art. IX).
- Any employee who prefers to stay out of K-1 land until conversion: their audited ledger entitlement is held as a **book entitlement that issues at conversion** as restricted stock. `[⚖ counsel: paper this so the §83 measurement date and the nominal-FMV window aren't lost — this is a design tension we flag rather than resolve; if the conversion happens within the same nominal-FMV window (the point of the 2-quarter deadline), the tax cost of deferring issuance to conversion is de minimis]`

## 4. Governance design

### 4.1 Why not five founder-managers

A five-founder management committee is the status quo that produced a year of drift. The evidence for small decision bodies: firm value declines with board size in the canonical public-company study ([Yermack 1996](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304405X95008445), N = 452 firms; correlational, public companies — external validity to startups is an inference), decision effectiveness drops ~10% per member added beyond seven ([Bain rule-of-7](https://www.bain.com/insights/effective-decision-making-and-the-rule-of-7/); proprietary consulting research), and actual startup norms seat **at most two founders** on a 3-person seed board regardless of founder count ([Rho](https://www.rho.co/blog/startup-board-composition); [Carta](https://carta.com/learn/startups/private-companies/board-of-directors/): most private boards run 3–9; [YC's Series A template](https://www.ycombinator.com/series_a_term_sheet) is 2 common + 1 preferred).

### 4.2 The Board: 3 seats — CEO, elected Active-Founder, independent chair

- **CEO seat (ex officio).** Accountability follows authority.
- **Active-Founder seat**, elected annually one-vote-per-Active-Founder. This preserves founder voice without giving every founder a chair — and inactive founders naturally fall out of the electorate (defined by the Active Service Standard, §5.3, not by anyone's discretion — freeze-out-resistant by objectivity).
- **Independent chair**, elected by all voting units, quorum-critical for reserved board matters. An independent tie-breaker is a standard deadlock device for closely held companies ([LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/deadlock-resolution-clause-procedures-buyouts-and-tax/); [SPZ Legal](https://spzlegal.com/blog/incorporation/how-to-resolve-deadlock-in-50-50-founder-situations)), and seating one *now* — before investors force it — is a costly signal of governance seriousness. It also pre-answers the empirical founder-control problem: each additional level of founder control (CEO seat + board control) is associated with 17–22% lower company valuation ([Wasserman, "Throne vs. Kingdom," *SMJ* 38(2), 2017](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/smj.2478), N = 6,130 startups; correlational), and founders who ceded both the CEO seat and board control showed the largest value premium in the underlying rich-vs-king data ([Wasserman 2006 working paper](https://web.mit.edu/iandeseminar/Papers/Fall2006/Wasserman.pdf), N = 457).

At a priced Series A this becomes the conventional 2-2-1 or YC-style 2-1 board ([Phoenix Strategy Group](https://www.phoenixstrategy.group/blog/board-composition-term-sheets-key-factors); [NVCA model documents](https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents/)); the operating agreement pre-drafts the expansion.

### 4.3 Decision rights: three tiers

1. **CEO decides** day-to-day within the Board-approved budget and plan.
2. **Board decides** (majority incl. independent for reserved board matters): budget, Rate Card, senior hires/fires, CEO appointment/removal, advisor agreements, litigation, related-party matters — the standard menu ([LegalGPS on LLC material decisions](https://www.legalgps.com/llc-operating-agreement/material-decisions)).
3. **Members decide by 66⅔% supermajority**: OA amendment, issuance outside the system, merger/sale/dissolution, debt above threshold, changes to ledger formulas — mirroring NVCA protective-provision patterns so the eventual investor negotiation is a swap-in, not a rebuild ([NVCA 2020 model term sheet](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/NVCA-2020-Term-Sheet.docx); supermajority norms of 66–75% for dilution/admission/sale, [LegalGPS](https://www.legalgps.com/llc-operating-agreement/material-decisions)).

Massachusetts default rules make explicit drafting essential: absent an operating agreement, management vests in members ([c. 156C §24](https://law.justia.com/codes/massachusetts/part-i/title-xxii/chapter-156c/section-24/)) and voting defaults to *contribution value*, not per capita ([§21](https://malegislature.gov/laws/generallaws/parti/titlexxii/chapter156c/section21)) — under the current handshake regime, nobody can even say who controls the company. The Act's freedom of contract ([§63](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXII/Chapter156C/Section63) — duties "may be expanded or restricted" by the OA) permits this whole design, but counsel should *not* attempt wholesale duty waivers: good faith remains the backstop ([Romano Law](https://www.romanolaw.com/can-fiduciary-obligations-be-waived-in-massachusetts/)), and SJC freeze-out doctrine (§2, principle 8) supplies the outer boundary.

### 4.4 Leadership: one CEO, chosen by structured contest, removable by the Board

**Why a single CEO.** Startup practitioner consensus is unambiguous — the CEO is the final decision-maker, and co-CEO structures usually mean founders were afraid to have the hard conversation ([Pilot's cofounder guide](https://pilot.com/tactical-guide/cofounders)). The counter-evidence is real but inapposite: public-company co-CEOs slightly outperformed peers in an 87-firm sample, yet fewer than 100 of 2,200 large firms ever tried it and success was conditional on pre-agreed role splits and dispute mechanisms ([HBR 2022](https://hbr.org/2022/07/is-it-time-to-consider-co-ceos); [Arena, Ferris & Unlu, *Financial Review* 46(3), 2011](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1759477) — peer-reviewed, heavily selected public-firm samples). For a five-engineer team that has already drifted for a year, a single accountable CEO is the defensible choice; we present the disagreement rather than hide it.

**Selection.** Open candidacy among active founders and senior participants; written 12-month operating plans; Board interview and majority vote with candidate-managers abstaining on themselves (OA §8.2). A contest decided on plans, not seniority, is the identical-terms principle applied to leadership.

**Removal.** By majority of the non-CEO managers, any time, with a documented process (notice; for alleged-cause performance issues, a 30-day cure period, crimes/fraud excluded — standard executive-agreement mechanics per [SEC-filed exemplars](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1026743/000102674315000027/a101ceoemploymentagreement.htm) and [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/can-a-board-of-directors-fire-a-ceo/)). Removal as CEO does not touch earned equity or member status — separating the job from the property is what makes removal usable. Given that four of five founder-CEO exits are forced and most founders are surprised ([Wasserman, HBR 2008](https://hbr.org/2008/02/the-founders-dilemma)), agreeing these mechanics now, symmetrically, before anyone knows who the CEO will be, is a Rawlsian bargain the whole team can sign.

### 4.5 Founder roles and performance measurement

Every Active Founder gets a written, Board-approved role with quarterly objectives agreed with the CEO (OA §8.4), reviewed annually in writing. This does triple duty: it feeds the set-aside earning test (§5.3), it creates the paper trail Massachusetts fiduciary law rewards when hard decisions later need a "legitimate business purpose" ([Wilkes](https://law.justia.com/cases/massachusetts/supreme-court/1976/370-mass-842-2.html)), and it is the professionalization step VCs otherwise impose ([Hellmann & Puri 2002](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1540-6261.00419)).

### 4.6 Deadlock

The ladder (OA Art. X): defined trigger → principal negotiation (10 days) → AAA mediation (30 days) → for ledger/valuation disputes, **final-offer ("baseball") expert determination**; for true member-level impasse, an **appraised-value buyout** option. This follows the standard practitioner menu ([ABA Business Law Today](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2017-march/deadlock-breaking-mechanisms/); [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/deadlock-resolution-clause-procedures-buyouts-and-tax/)). We deliberately **exclude shotgun/Texas-shootout clauses**: they presume two parties and equal wallets, and the capital-poor side can be forced to sell below value because it cannot finance the counter-purchase on the clause's timeline ([Farrell Fritz](https://www.nybusinessdivorce.com/2017/05/articles/llcs/aim-carefully-pulling-trigger-shotgun-buy-sell-agreement/); [Peckar & Abramson](https://www.pecklaw.com/business-owner-rights/shotgun-buy-sell-option-agreements/)) — a fatal defect in a 15-holder company of unpaid engineers. The backstop everyone should want to avoid: Massachusetts judicial dissolution requires showing it is "not reasonably practicable to carry on" the business ([c. 156C §44](https://law.justia.com/codes/massachusetts/part-i/title-xxii/chapter-156c/section-44/)), a standard courts apply reluctantly (cf. the corporate "true deadlock" framework of [Koshy v. Sachdev, 477 Mass. 759 (2017)](http://masscases.com/cases/sjc/477/477mass759.html)) — meaning a deadlocked company most likely *stays* deadlocked unless the contract provides an exit.

## 5. The equity system

### 5.1 The blocks (10,000,000 units)

| Block | % | Governing mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Founder Reserves — 5 × 5% | 25% | Prospective quarterly earn-in, use-it-or-lose-it (§5.3) |
| Contribution Pool | 65% | Points ledger, identical terms for founders and employees (§5.4–5.7) |
| Advisor Pool | 2% | Written advisor agreements; includes the professor's 1.0% (§7) |
| Unissued buffer | 8% | Board-controlled; issuable only through the same mechanisms |

Calibration sanity-check against market: the 65% + forfeiture inflows functions as compensation for *everyone* — the five founders' variable component plus what is effectively a very large employee pool. Ordinary companies carve 10–20% for employees ([Index Ventures](https://www.indexventures.com/rewarding-talent/esop-size); [Holloway](https://www.holloway.com/g/equity-compensation/sections/the-option-pool)) — but ordinary companies pay salaries; here the entire team worked a year unpaid, and median grants for hires #1–#5 alone run 1.49%→0.34% *with* salary ([Carta first-10-hires data](https://community.carta.com/c/corporations-updates/equity-for-your-first-8-hires)). In the worked example (§5.5), employees collectively earn ~34.6% of the company for Year 1 — far above market for paid teams, defensibly at-market for a team that funded the company with forgone wages. Founders who stay active land at 12–16% each (Year-1 ledger + set-aside + future accrual), within sight of the Carta medians for founding teams at comparable maturity (median founding-team total ~56% post-seed, across usually 2–3 founders — [Carta Founder Ownership Report](https://carta.com/data/founder-ownership/)); five-founder teams individually hold less by arithmetic necessity.

### 5.2 Why this architecture and not the two pure alternatives

- **Pure Slicing Pie** (fully dynamic percentages until funding) maximizes fairness-tracking but is the version investors distrust: a cap table that is a moving spreadsheet ([Equity Matrix critique](https://equitymatrix.io/blog/slicing-pie-problems); [Sentient Law](https://www.sentientlaw.com/dynamic-equity-slicing-pie/)). We keep its valuation engine (market-rate hours, risk multipliers) but bound dynamism *inside a fixed 65% pool* and freeze at financing.
- **Pure static split with vesting** (the Demmler [Founders' Pie Calculator](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/fd0n/35%20Founders'%20Pie%20Calculator.htm) one-shot negotiation, then 4-year vesting) is simpler but cannot satisfy fixed requirement 2 — employees and founders competing on identical ongoing terms — and re-creates the stickiness problem the team already has ([Hellmann et al. 2019](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/sej.1328)).
- **Honesty note:** there is **no peer-reviewed empirical evaluation of any dynamic-equity framework**. The academic support is indirect — vesting theory ([Hellmann & Thiele 2015](https://academic.oup.com/jleo/article-abstract/31/3/629/808939)), the justice findings ([Breugst et al. 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883902614000676)), and evidence that renegotiation doesn't happen without a forcing mechanism ([Hellmann et al. 2019](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/sej.1328)). The choice of this architecture is judgment on top of that evidence, and we say so.

### 5.3 The founder set-asides (fixed requirement 1, made mechanical)

Each founder's 500,000-unit (5%) Reserve earns in **12 quarterly tranches over 3 years**, each tranche earned if and only if that quarter's **Active Service Standard** was met: ≥400 ledger-verified hours (≈31 hrs/week; ≥300 for a Board-pre-approved reduced-load role) **and** ≥70% of written quarterly objectives, certified with the founder recused. Missed tranches are **forfeited permanently into the Contribution Pool** — no catch-up. Three consecutive missed quarters ⇒ remaining Reserve forfeits and the founder is re-designated Inactive.

Design choices, argued:
- **Quarterly earn-in with no cliff** (vs. the standard 1-year cliff): these founders already served their "cliff year"; market practice explicitly credits pre-agreement service via back-dated vesting commencement ([Yokum Taku](https://www.startupcompanylawyer.com/2007/07/19/what-should-the-vesting-terms-of-founder-stock-be-before-a-venture-financing/); [Flux Law](https://www.flux.law/blog/founder-vesting-explained): 6–12 months' credit typical). Here the credit is delivered more precisely — through the retroactive ledger — so the Reserve itself starts fresh and fully prospective.
- **3 years, not 4 or 8.** The Reserve is only the *loyalty* component (≤5%); the ledger already handles pay-for-work continuously. A shorter, conditional schedule concentrates retention where the data says risk concentrates — 50% founder-CEO attrition by year 3 ([Wasserman 2008](https://hbr.org/2008/02/the-founders-dilemma)), ~65% of companies losing a co-founder pre-Series B ([SaaStr/Carta](https://www.saastr.com/carta-co-founder-break-ups-are-accelerating)). (The 8-year-vest school — [Jolis](https://techcrunch.com/2020/10/17/solve-the-dead-equity-problem-with-a-longer-founder-vesting-schedule/) — attacks dead equity, which this design already prevents by paying in arrears.)
- **Performance condition on top of time.** A pure time vest would let a coasting founder collect 5% for attendance, violating "earned, use-it-or-lose-it." The dual test (hours from the ledger + objectives from §8.4) keeps the condition objective and paper-trailed.
- **Modest single-trigger acceleration (4 tranches) on sale for then-active founders; earned-only otherwise.** Investors and acquirers strongly prefer double-trigger and view broad single-trigger skeptically ([Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/what-are-single-and-double-trigger-acceleration-and-how-do-they-work/)); the OA flags this as a negotiable point.

### 5.4 The Contribution Ledger — full specification (fixed requirement 2)

**The formula.** For participant *i* in month *m*:

```
Points(i,m) = Σ_activities [ Hours × HourlyRate(function, level) × CompMultiplier × QualityFactor ]
            + 4.0 × CashContributed(i,m)
            + 2.0 × BoardApprovedPropertyValue(i,m)

HourlyRate(function, level) = RateCard annual salary ÷ 2,080
CompMultiplier               = 1 + (1 − f),  f = fraction of Rate-Card cash actually paid
                               (unpaid work ⇒ 2.0; fully paid work ⇒ 1.0)
QualityFactor                = 0.8 | 1.0 | up to 1.2 per published rubric
Entitlement share of pool    = Σ Points(i) ÷ Σ Points(all)      [recomputed quarterly]
Units(i)                     = Entitlement share × 6,500,000 (plus forfeiture inflows)
```

**Multiplier provenance.** The 2× non-cash / 4× cash risk multipliers are Slicing Pie's, adopted as the only widely used convention ([slicingpie.com](https://slicingpie.com/the-grunt-fund-calculator/)); they are a practitioner heuristic, not an evidence-backed constant, and the OA locks them so they can only change by supermajority. The linear interpolation on partial pay (our addition) removes the cliff between "unpaid" and "paid" once salaries start.

**How coding vs. management vs. vision are valued — the engineering-team problem.** Hours are priced at the market rate of the **function performed that month**, from a Board-adopted Rate Card (median Boston-metro base salary per function/level from ≥2 independent published compensation datasets, refreshed annually — the same benchmark-driven approach the major comp datasets support ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/equity-report-2024/); [Index OptionPlan](https://www.indexventures.com/optionplan/), used here for method, with actual numbers set at adoption). So a founder who managed three engineers half-time and coded half-time earns EM-rate points on the first half and SWE-rate points on the second — same as an employee doing the same work. Two guards make "vision" creditable without becoming a founder tax on the pool:
- **Artifact requirement:** strategy/vision hours count only against a written, dated artifact (strategy memo, executed partnership, shipped hiring plan).
- **The 15% cap:** vision hours are capped at 15% of any person's quarterly hours unless the Board pre-approves a designated role (CEO up to 50%). Rationale: high-level "vision" is the contribution category most susceptible to self-serving inflation and least verifiable ex post; the cap forces the default valuation of an engineering company's time toward its verifiable engineering output. The cap number itself is judgment — no literature prices vision hours — and is flagged as an open calibration decision.

**Verification.** Monthly logs tied to artifacts (commits, PRs, tickets, docs, attended meetings); certification within 30 days by a Compensation Committee of the independent manager + two elected participants (at most one founder); Quality Factor ≠ 1.0 requires written justification; 60-hour/week and 260-hour/month caps; stale uncertified logs lapse. Process visibility is the point: the Breugst mechanism (§1b) says the *legitimacy* of the split is what protects the team, and legitimacy is manufactured by auditable process.

### 5.6 The retroactive year (Year-1 Retrospective Contribution Audit)

One-time, 90-day process on the same formulas (OA §6.7): evidence-backed claimed logs (git history is unusually good evidence for an engineering team — commits, PR reviews, design docs, calendars); 60-hour/week plausibility cap; hours lacking artifact corroboration for ≥60% of the claim credited at 50%; calibrated single-session scoring by the Compensation Committee with founders recused from their own claims; published results; 15-day objection window into the dispute ladder; final balances expressly supersede all prior informal understandings, with mutual releases. The 2.0× multiplier applies to substantially all Year-1 time (nobody was paid). Legal handling of the associated tax event is §3.2 Step 1; **counsel must sequence the releases with the Wage Act remediation (§10.1) — equity cannot legally settle wage claims** ([c. 149 §148's special-contract bar](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section148)).

Why audit rather than negotiate: an open multi-party renegotiation of a stale handshake is precisely the fairness-norm minefield the renegotiation literature documents ([Hellmann et al. 2019](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/sej.1328)); converting it into an evidence-scoring exercise under pre-agreed formulas turns a zero-sum fight into a clerical process with an appeal path.

### 5.7 Ongoing tracking and the freeze

Monthly logging (≤30 min/person against artifacts), monthly certification, quarterly unit true-ups, annual Rate Card refresh. The ledger **freezes at the first qualified financing** (or sale, or supermajority vote): final entitlements issue, the unissued buffer becomes the conventional option pool, and compensation flips to salary + forward-vesting grants sized to market ([Carta hire benchmarks](https://community.carta.com/c/corporations-updates/equity-for-your-first-8-hires); [Holloway levels](https://www.holloway.com/g/equity-compensation/sections/typical-employee-equity-levels)). Investors then see a static, fully documented cap table plus a Rate-Card-driven history any analyst can recompute (OA §14.3) — converting the system's obligation for transparency into a diligence asset instead of the "spreadsheet cap table" liability the critics warn about ([Equity Matrix](https://equitymatrix.io/blog/slicing-pie-problems)).

## 6. Inactive founders

The design's answer, in one line: **inactive founders keep every unit they earned, stop earning the moment they stop contributing, and hold no reserved power — and nothing about their status requires anyone to make a discretionary (litigable) decision.**

- **Equity:** earned Class C from the audit and any earned Reserve tranches are theirs outright (never confiscated); unearned Reserve tranches lapse automatically by the objective hours/objectives test; their pool share passively dilutes as active people accrue points. Result in the worked example: F4 stabilizes near ~1.6% — real compensation for real early work, and far below the ~10% dead-equity red-flag threshold ([ISA Ventures](https://isaventures.substack.com/p/dead-equity-on-your-cap-table-is)).
- **Roles/board:** no ex-officio anything; the Active-Founder board seat electorate is defined by the same objective standard.
- **Voting:** earned units vote (one unit, one vote). Stripping votes from earned units would invite exactly the freeze-out claims Massachusetts law is famous for ([Pointer](http://masscases.com/cases/sjc/455/455mass537.html); [Allison](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/spr-jud-crt-mas-suf/1896955.html)); instead, inactive holders' voting weight declines naturally with relative dilution.
- **Exit paths:** voluntary sale via ROFR; the deadlock-triggered appraised-value buyout; c. 156C §36 resignation (with the OA's written-agreement override of the [§32 fair-value payout default](https://law.justia.com/codes/massachusetts/part-i/title-xxii/chapter-156c/section-32/)). No forced redemption for mere inactivity — the OA's only involuntary call is for adjudicated covenant breach, calibrated with counsel to avoid penalty-clause and fiduciary problems.
- **Retention logic, not just fairness:** founder departure propensity falls with ownership and board membership ([Boeker & Karichalil, *AMJ* 45(4), 2002](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/3069314)); a semi-active founder who can still earn by contributing (identical ledger terms) has a live incentive to re-engage rather than litigate.

## 7. The professor

**The problem.** An oral promise of "a decent share" at "a higher rate than others," to a part-time Yale ML professor. Legally: likely unenforceable as a contract for indefiniteness, yet a live quantum-meruit/promissory-estoppel exposure for the value of services rendered ([case-law commentary on a materially identical handshake-advisor dispute](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/ensuring-definiteness-in-oral-agreements-and-viability-of-quantum-meruit-claims-under-california-law/view); [Schlam Stone on quantum meruit surviving the statute of frauds](https://www.schlamstone.com/blogs/commercial/2020-10-17-statute-of-frauds-does-not-bar-quantum-meruit-claims/); practitioner consensus that informal equity promises are the seed of most startup equity litigation — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/legal-exchange-insights-and-commentary/how-startup-founders-can-avoid-disputes-that-turn-into-lawsuits)).

**The market answer.** Part-time advisor equity is small: Carta medians of **0.21% at pre-seed** with only ~10% of pre-seed advisors at ≥1% ([Carta advisory-shares data](https://carta.com/learn/startups/equity-management/advisory-shares/)); the FAST framework tops out at **1.0%** for expert-level engagement at the earliest stage on 2-year monthly vesting ([fi.co/fast](https://fi.co/fast)); biotech SAB norms run 0.1–0.3% plus cash ([LifeSciVC](https://lifescivc.com/2012/09/biotech-scientific-advisory-boards-what-works-what-doesnt/)); law-firm guidance flags >1% single-advisor grants as a recurring regret ([WilmerHale](https://launch.wilmerhale.com/explore/formation/equity-incentives/advisory-board-setup-and-compensation)). "1–2% for a founding-level scientific advisor" exists only at the extreme upper tail ([Fifty Years spinout playbook](https://fiftyyears.com/spinout); [Osage](https://oup.vc/insights/solving-the-equity-equation/)).

**The offer (OA §7.2): 1.0% (100,000 units), 24-month monthly vesting, conditioned on ≥8 hours/month, in full settlement of the promise, with a release.** This is the top of the documented market for his role — defensible to him as generous and to future investors as benchmarked.

**His "higher rate" expectation, honored without corrupting the system.** Fixed requirement 2 forbids premium *rates* inside the ledger for founders and employees; the professor sits outside it as an advisor, and his premium arrives through the fixed grant's arithmetic rather than a special rate: 100,000 units for ~192 hours (24 × 8) ≈ **520 units/hour**, versus ≈ 305 units/hour earned by a senior (L3) engineer in the Year-1 example (79.33 × 2.0 = 158.66 points/hr × ~1.92 units/point) — roughly **1.7× the effective per-hour equity of the team's senior engineers**, visible in one line of arithmetic. If he wants more upside, he may enroll in the ledger *prospectively at standard rates* like everyone else. Negotiation fallback (pre-authorized to the Board): up to 1.5% total on a 36-month schedule with a scoped deliverable (e.g., named involvement in fundraising and recruiting); beyond that, walk — at 2%+ the grant exceeds documented norms and starts to read as dead equity in diligence.

**The Yale overlay (all flagged for counsel and for his disclosure process):** Yale caps external professional activity at one day in seven and **prohibits involving Yale students, employees, or postdocs** in the outside engagement ([Yale Provost guidance](https://provost.yale.edu/policies/external-professional-activities-guidance)); its COI policy requires annual disclosure and Provost-level review for startup entanglements ([Yale COI policy](https://research-support.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2025-03/coi_policy_0.pdf)); the Patent Policy obligates assignment of inventions to Yale ([Yale Patent Policy](https://ventures.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2023-03/Yale_Patent_Policy.pdf)) — and Yale has litigated faculty who routed inventions around it, winning ~$1M against a Nobel laureate ([Fenn v. Yale, 283 F. Supp. 2d 615 (D. Conn. 2003)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/283/615/2576942/)). The advisor agreement therefore must include: IP assignment scoped to company work; representations of no use of Yale resources, facilities, funding, students, or research results; evidence of COI disclosure; and a defined time commitment comfortably inside the one-in-seven cap — the standard structure for university-affiliated advisors ([Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/engaging-advisors-from-universities-and-research-institutes-what-you-should-know/)). If his ML advice risks overlapping his Yale research agenda, counsel should assess whether any needed IP must instead be licensed from Yale — a chain-of-title question investors will diligence ([Orrick on university spinouts](https://www.orrick.com/en/insights/2025/02/university-spinouts-the-risks-and-benefits)).

## 8. Employees

- **Year 1:** full retroactive participation in the audit on identical terms — in the worked example employees collectively earn ~34.6% of the company, and the top employee out-earns two founders. This is the honest price of a year of unpaid work by ten people, and it is the design's loudest credibility signal to the team.
- **Ongoing:** same ledger, same rates, same committee. New hires enroll by Board approval with a role/level assignment.
- **After the freeze:** conventional market-rate cash + equity ([Carta benchmarks](https://community.carta.com/c/corporations-updates/equity-for-your-first-8-hires); [Index OptionPlan](https://www.indexventures.com/optionplan/)), from the pool created by the unissued buffer.
- **Immediately and regardless of equity:** the Wage Act problem (§10.1) must be remediated — equity participation does not repair it and cannot waive it.

## 9. Investor-readiness summary

1. **Clean story at financing:** frozen ledger → static cap table; every holder's position recomputable from records (OA §14.3); no dead equity by construction; founder set-asides convert to standard unvested restricted stock ([Cooley GO reverse-vesting frame](https://www.cooleygo.com/founder-basics-founders-stock/)).
2. **Standard governance to swap into NVCA docs:** 3-seat board expands to 2-1 or 2-2-1 ([YC template](https://www.ycombinator.com/series_a_term_sheet); [NVCA](https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents/)); supermajority list maps onto protective provisions.
3. **Entity:** Delaware C-corp before institutional money, QSBS clock running (§3.2).
4. **Pre-consented conversion + drag-along** already signed by every holder (OA §§11.2, 14.2) — no holdout risk from 15+ holders, which is itself a diligence talking point given VC allergy to crowded, opinionated cap tables ([SeedLegals](https://seedlegals.com/resources/messy-cap-table/); [SVB](https://www.svb.com/startup-insights/startup-equity/understanding-startup-cap-table/)).
5. **Founder-control posture:** independent chair from day one; the team can cite the control-vs-value evidence ([Wasserman 2017](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/smj.2478)) as its reason — a governance story most seed companies cannot tell.

## 12. Where the evidence is weak, mixed, or absent — stated plainly

1. **No dynamic-equity framework has peer-reviewed outcome evidence.** Slicing Pie and the Founders' Pie Calculator are practitioner artifacts; our adoption of their mechanics rests on indirect academic support (vesting theory, justice findings) plus judgment.
2. **The equal-split penalty is correlational.** Hellmann & Wasserman note equal splitting may partly signal weak teams rather than cause weak outcomes; nothing here relies on the causal reading — the design fixes process, which is beneficial under either interpretation.
3. **Team-size evidence conflicts.** Larger, diverse founding teams predicted faster growth in semiconductor ventures ([Eisenhardt & Schoonhoven 1990](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1446661cd44d1b75d90098453822e909395127a3)), while solo founders showed better survival in a crowdfunding sample ([Greenberg & Mollick, SSRN working paper](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3107898) — not peer-reviewed as far as we could verify). We draw no conclusion about the "right" number of founders; the design manages the five that exist.
4. **Co-CEO evidence is mixed** (§4.4): public-company samples mildly favorable under strict conditions; startup practitioner consensus strongly against. We chose a single CEO and show both sides.
5. **The 65% "people problems" failure statistic is book-level, not journal-level** — cited only as such.
6. **Calibration constants are judgment:** the 2×/4× multipliers, the 15% vision cap, the 400-hour quarterly threshold, the 0.8–1.2 quality band, 66⅔% supermajority, and the 65/25/2/8 block sizes have no empirical literature behind their precise values; each is locked against casual drift (supermajority to amend) and listed as a bracketed decision for the founders and counsel.
7. **Verification limitation:** all URLs search-verified rather than fetch-verified (network policy); per-source notes in `plan-2-references.md`.
8. **Isolation compliance:** no other team's branch or files were read, checked out, diffed, or browsed at any point; work occurred exclusively on orphan branch `plan-2`.
