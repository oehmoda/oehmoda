# References

Consolidated bibliography for the [one-pager](founder-governance-and-equity-one-pager.md) and [implementation plan](implementation-plan.md). Inline citations in those documents use author–year keys that match the entries below. Each entry notes what it is cited for and, where relevant, how strong the evidence is. The full research briefs (with additional detail per source) are in [`research/`](research/).

## A. Peer-reviewed and academic sources

1. **Hellmann, T. & Wasserman, N. (2016).** "The First Deal: The Division of Founder Equity in New Ventures." *Management Science* 63(8): 2647–2666. [Publisher](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2474) · [NBER WP 16922](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16922) · [NBER Digest summary](https://www.nber.org/digest/aug11/division-founder-equity-new-ventures) · [Extended HBS WP 14-085](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/14-085_2bd67a49-bd41-4396-a69d-73a7f40829b8.pdf)
   *Cited for:* ~33% of teams split equally; 42% decide in a day or less ("quick handshake"); 73% split within the first month; ~half of teams have no vesting/adjustment terms; renegotiation rarely succeeds because the over-allocated founder holds a veto; larger teams agree less easily. **Evidence: strong** (1,476–3,782 founders across survey waves), though the fundraising penalty for equal splits is selection, not causation — the authors say so themselves.

2. **Wasserman, N. (2012).** *The Founder's Dilemmas: Anticipating and Avoiding the Pitfalls That Can Sink a Startup.* Princeton University Press. [Publisher](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691158303/the-founders-dilemmas)
   *Cited for:* the ~65% of high-potential startup failures attributed to co-founder/people problems; teams avoiding the equity "elephant in the room"; vesting as a commitment device; 4 of 5 founders resisting replacement as the company scales. **Caveat:** the 65% figure comes from Wasserman's HBS dataset analysis, not a refereed failure study — treat as a credible expert estimate, not a hard number ([Entrepreneur coverage](https://www.entrepreneur.com/leadership/harvard-business-school-professor-says-65-of-startups-fail/370367), [CNN Money](https://money.cnn.com/2014/02/24/smallbusiness/startups-entrepreneur-cofounder/)).

3. **Wasserman, N. (2017).** "The Throne vs. the Kingdom: Founder Control and Value Creation in Startups." *Strategic Management Journal* 38(2): 255–277. [Publisher](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/smj.2478)
   *Cited for:* on 6,130 US startups, each level of founder control retained (board control and/or CEO seat) is associated with 17.1–22.0% lower pre-money valuation — the "rich vs. king" tradeoff. **Evidence: strong sample, correlational** — founders who cede control differ from those who don't.

4. **Wasserman, N. (2008).** "The Founder's Dilemma." *Harvard Business Review*, Feb 2008. [HBR](https://hbr.org/2008/02/the-founders-dilemma)
   *Cited for:* the rich-vs-king framing and the case for a single accountable leader.

5. **Breugst, N., Patzelt, H. & Rathgeber, P. (2015).** "How Should We Divide the Pie? Equity Distribution and Its Impact on Entrepreneurial Teams." *Journal of Business Venturing* 30(1): 66–94. [Abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbvent/v30y2015i1p66-94.html)
   *Cited for:* *perceived justice* of the split — not equality per se — drives positive vs. negative team-interaction spirals. **Evidence: small-N longitudinal qualitative (8 teams)** — strong on mechanism, not statistically generalizable.

6. **Hellmann, T. & Thiele, V. (2012/2015).** "Contracting Among Founders." SSRN WP; later *Journal of Law, Economics & Organization*. [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1968696)
   *Cited for:* formal theory of early vs. delayed founder contracting; delay/contingent terms are more attractive when uncertainty about relative contribution is high — the theoretical basis for dynamic/deferred allocation.

7. **Klotz, A., Hmieleski, K., Bradley, B. & Busenitz, L. (2014).** "New Venture Teams: A Review of the Literature and Roadmap for Future Research." *Journal of Management* 40(1): 226–255. [Publisher](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0149206313493325)
   *Cited for:* team-founded ventures generally outperform solo ones; benefits of added members are offset by coordination and conflict costs; no robust evidence for an "optimal" team size.

8. **Ruef, M., Aldrich, H. & Carter, N. (2003).** "The Structure of Founding Teams." *American Sociological Review* 68. [Context](https://www.oxfordbibliographies.com/display/document/obo-9780199846740/obo-9780199846740-0218.xml)
   *Cited for:* founding teams form by homophily and strong ties (friends, family, coworkers), not competence complementarity — predisposing them to relationship-based conflict.

9. **Kotha, R. & George, G. (2012).** "Friends, Family, or Fools: Entrepreneur Experience and Its Implications for Equity Distribution and Resource Mobilization." *Journal of Business Venturing* 27(5): 525–543. [Citation context](https://iaap-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/apps.12515)
   *Cited for:* experienced founders allocate equity more selectively; contribution- and experience-driven allocation patterns.

10. **Choi, J., Goldschlag, N., Haltiwanger, J. & Kim, J.D. (2021).** "Founding Teams and Startup Performance." NBER WP 28417. [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28417)
    *Cited for:* causal evidence (via premature founder deaths, US administrative data) that founding-team human capital strongly drives startup size, survival, and productivity.

11. **Garg, S. (2013).** "Venture Boards: Distinctive Monitoring and Implications for Firm Performance." *Academy of Management Review* 38(1): 90–108. [Publisher](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/amr.2010.0193) · **Garg, S. & Furr, N. (2017).** *Strategic Entrepreneurship Journal*. [Publisher](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sej.1258)
    *Cited for:* venture boards differ fundamentally from public-company boards; monitoring intertwined with resource provision.

12. **Arthurs, J. & Busenitz, L. (2003).** "The Boundaries and Limitations of Agency Theory and Stewardship Theory in the Venture Capitalist/Entrepreneur Relationship." *Entrepreneurship Theory & Practice*. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227761037_The_Boundaries_and_Limitations_of_Agency_Theory_and_Stewardship_Theory_in_the_Venture_CapitalistEntrepreneur_Relationship)
    *Cited for:* neither pure agency nor pure stewardship theory fits startup governance.

13. **Donaldson, L. & Davis, J. (1991).** "Stewardship Theory or Agency Theory." *Australian Journal of Management* 16(1). [Publisher](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/031289629101600103)
    *Cited for:* the stewardship-vs-agency framing underlying board design choices.

14. **Kaplan, S. & Strömberg, P. (2003).** "Financial Contracting Theory Meets the Real World: An Empirical Analysis of Venture Capital Contracts." *Review of Economic Studies* 70(2).
    *Cited for:* investor-imposed vesting and control terms as standard in VC contracts.

15. **CB Insights (2014–2021).** "The Top Reasons Startups Fail." [Report](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/report/startup-failure-reasons-top/) · [PDF](https://s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/cbi-content/research-reports/The-20-Reasons-Startups-Fail.pdf)
    *Cited for:* "not the right team" in ~14–23% of self-reported post-mortems; market causes rank higher. **Evidence: non-academic, self-reported, non-random** — directional only; measures proximate causes where Wasserman measures underlying dynamics.

## B. Industry data and practitioner frameworks

16. **Seibel, M. (Y Combinator).** "How to Split Equity Among Co-Founders." [michaelseibel.com](https://www.michaelseibel.com/blog/how-to-split-equity-among-founders) · [YC Startup Library](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/5x-how-to-split-equity-among-co-founders)
    *Cited for:* YC's case for (near-)equal splits; motivation > points saved; 7–10 year company-building horizon; vesting (4-year/1-year cliff) — not unequal splits — as the protection against early departure.

17. **Carta (P. Walker).** "How do co-founders actually split equity?" and **Founder Ownership Report 2025**. [Split data](https://carta.com/data/how-co-founders-split-equity/) · [Ownership report](https://carta.com/data/founder-ownership/)
    *Cited for:* 45.9% of two-founder teams split equally in 2024 (vs 31.5% in 2015); option pools 10–15% at seed/Series A; first-hire median ~1.5% (eng ~2.0%); <5% total for first 10 hires; founder teams hold ~56% median post-seed. **Evidence: large platform dataset**, skewed to venture-track companies.

18. **Carta.** "Vesting Explained." [Guide](https://carta.com/learn/equity/stock-options/vesting/)
    *Cited for:* standard 4-year/1-year-cliff mechanics; single- vs double-trigger acceleration norms.

19. **Carta.** "Profits Interests." [Guide](https://carta.com/learn/startups/compensation/equity-incentive-plans/profits-interest/) · **Lyons Gaddis**, "Profits Interests in LLCs." [Article](https://www.lyonsgaddis.com/profits-interests-in-llcs-a-tax-friendly-alternative-to-stock-options/) · **The Startup Law Blog**, "Complete Guide to Equity Compensation." [Guide](https://www.thestartuplawblog.com/the-complete-guide-to-equity-compensation-for-startups/)
    *Cited for:* profits interests tax-free at grant under Rev. Proc. 93-27/2001-43; threshold-value mechanics; K-1/no-W-2 friction; no QSBS for LLC interests.

20. **Index Ventures (D. Jacquesson).** *Rewarding Talent* handbook and OptionPlan calculators. [Handbook](https://www.indexventures.com/rewarding-talent/) · [OptionPlan](https://www.indexventures.com/optionplan/)
    *Cited for:* employee-grant benchmarks by role/stage; ~10% ESOP at seed, 10–15% by Series A.

21. **Moyer, M. (2012/2016).** *Slicing Pie* / *The Slicing Pie Handbook*. [Multipliers](https://slicingpie.com/the-magic-of-mutipliers/) · [Freeze/recovery](https://support.thepieslicer.com/article/show/112619-how-do-i-freeze-bake-terminate-the-pie)
    *Cited for:* dynamic ("Grunt Fund") equity mechanics — slices = at-risk FMV × multiplier (2x non-cash, 4x cash); at-market pay accrues nothing; recovery framework on departure; freezing at financing/self-sufficiency. **Evidence: practitioner framework, widely adopted, no peer-reviewed validation.**

22. **Silicon Hills Lawyer (J.M. Escobar-Avila, 2015).** "Splitting Founder Equity: Avoid Grunt Funds." [Article](https://siliconhillslawyer.com/2015/06/01/founder-equity-grunt-funds/)
    *Cited for:* the standard legal critique of dynamic equity — tax/legal complexity in corporations, VC resistance — and why it fits LLCs pre-financing best.

23. **Shapiro, D. (2011).** "The Only Wrong Answer Is 50/50: Calculating the Co-Founder Equity Split." [Article](https://www.danshapiro.com/blog/2011/04/startup-cofounder-equity-split/)
    *Cited for:* the counter-case to equal splits: idea premium (~5%), CEO premium, full-time-vs-part-time discounts, cash as a note rather than equity.

24. **Founder Institute.** FAST Agreement (Founder/Advisor Standard Template). [FAST](https://fi.co/fast) · **Foundrs.com** equity calculator. [Calculator](https://foundrs.com/)
    *Cited for:* advisor-equity benchmarks (0.15–1.0% by tier); questionnaire-based contribution weighting.

25. **Capbase.** "Founder Vesting Schedules: Best Practices." [Article](https://capbase.com/founder-vesting-schedules-best-practices/)
    *Cited for:* reverse vesting, 83(b) timing, re-vesting demands at Series A, dead-equity diligence risk.

26. **Bird & Bird (2025).** "Leaver Provisions." [Article](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/leaver-provisions-the-terms-that-founders-fear-the-most) · **Ashfords.** "Leaver Provisions: Balancing Founder Fairness and Company Protection." [Article](https://www.ashfords.co.uk/insights/blog/leaver-provisions-balancing-founder-fairness-and-company-protection)
    *Cited for:* good-leaver/bad-leaver mechanics (FMV vs cost buybacks); US practice favoring vesting + ROFR over punitive vested-share clawbacks.

## C. Governance, decision-making, and leadership

27. **Horowitz, B. (a16z).** "Peacetime CEO/Wartime CEO" and *The Hard Thing About Hard Things* (2014). [Article](https://a16z.com/peacetime-ceo-wartime-ceo/)
    *Cited for:* someone must decide with imperfect information; judge executives on their organization's output.

28. **Point Nine Capital.** "Can a Startup Have Two CEOs?" [Article](https://medium.com/point-nine-news/can-a-startup-have-two-ceos-22eadd040d5e) · **Gil, E.** "Unequal Cofounders." [Article](https://blog.eladgil.com/p/unequal-cofounders)
    *Cited for:* investor rejection of co-CEO/consensus structures; shared power making startups slower and less aggressive.

29. **Y Combinator / Graham, P.** "The 18 Mistakes That Kill Startups"; YC founder-breakup data. [Forbes summary](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jodiecook/2025/01/06/the-18-common-mistakes-that-kill-startups-according-to-y-combinator/)
    *Cited for:* founder fights as a canonical startup killer; ~20% of YC startups lose a founder.

30. **NYU Entrepreneurship.** "Startup Board Structure." [Article](https://entrepreneur.nyu.edu/blog/2026/07/20/startup-board-structure/) · **Rho.** "Startup Board Composition." [Article](https://www.rho.co/blog/startup-board-composition) · **Shockwave Innovations.** "Early Stage Board Composition." [Article](https://shockwaveinnovations.com/early-stage-board-composition/)
    *Cited for:* stage-by-stage board norms (founders-only 1–3 pre-seed; 2-1 at seed; 2-2-1 at Series A); avoid oversized/even boards.

31. **CRV.** "What Is a Co-Founder?" [Article](https://www.crv.com/content/what-is-a-co-founder) · **Pilot.** "Tactical Guide: Cofounders." [Guide](https://pilot.com/tactical-guide/cofounders)
    *Cited for:* dividing roles by real strength; DRI mapping; title-inflation risk at 4–5 founders.

32. **HSG Capital.** "Founder to CEO Transition." [Article](https://www.hsgcap.com/article/founder-to-ceo-transition/)
    *Cited for:* the founder-as-bottleneck signal and pre-committed role transitions.

33. **Doerr, J. (2018).** *Measure What Matters.* [Betterworks summary](https://www.betterworks.com/magazine/doerr-reveals-his-company-management-secrets-in-measure-what-matters)
    *Cited for:* executive/founder OKRs; CFRs; decoupling goal attainment from compensation (hence: vest on time, not metrics).

## D. Legal — LLC structure and Massachusetts

34. **American Bar Association.** "LLC Agreement Checklist." *GPSolo eReport* (2022). [Checklist](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/gpsolo/publications/gpsolo_ereport/2022/july-2022/llc-agreement-checklist/)
    *Cited for:* tiered voting structures (discretion → majority → supermajority → unanimity); reserved-matters design.

35. **LegalClarity.** "LLC Supermajority Voting." [Article](https://legalclarity.org/llc-supermajority-voting-for-amendments-how-it-works/) · **LegalGPS.** "Material Decisions in Operating Agreements." [Article](https://www.legalgps.com/llc-operating-agreement/material-decisions)
    *Cited for:* typical supermajority thresholds (66.7–80%) and decision lists; keep unanimity lists short.

36. **Massachusetts General Laws c. 156C** (Limited Liability Company Act), esp. §2(9) (agreement may be oral), §11 (good-faith reliance on written agreement), §63 (duties may be expanded or restricted). [§63 text](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXII/Chapter156c/Section63)
    *Cited for:* MA statutory framework for the operating agreement and fiduciary-duty tailoring.

37. **Romano Law.** "Can Fiduciary Obligations Be Waived in Massachusetts?" [Article](https://www.romanolaw.com/can-fiduciary-obligations-be-waived-in-massachusetts/)
    *Cited for:* MA's strict close-corporation duties (*Donahue* line) and the value of express §63 modification.

38. **Thomson Reuters Practical Law.** "Transfer Provisions in LLC Agreements." [Article](https://legal.thomsonreuters.com/en/insights/articles/transfer-provisions-in-llc-agreements)
    *Cited for:* ROFR, tag-along, drag-along mechanics.

39. **The Jacobs Law (MA).** "Preventing Business Standstills: Deadlock Clauses." [Article](https://thejacobslaw.com/preventing-business-standstills/) · **LegalClarity.** "Texas Shootout Clause." [Article](https://legalclarity.org/texas-shootout-clause-how-it-works-risks-and-tax-rules/)
    *Cited for:* deadlock escalation ladders; shotgun and Texas-shootout buy-sell mechanics and their cash-position bias.

40. **Beck Reed Riden.** "Quick Guide to Massachusetts' Noncompete Law" (G.L. c. 149 §24L). [Guide](https://beckreedriden.com/quick-guide-to-massachusetts-new-noncompete-law/)
    *Cited for:* MA garden-leave/12-month requirements for employment noncompetes and the sale-of-business/owner exemption.

41. **UpCounsel.** "LLC Board of Managers." [Article](https://www.upcounsel.com/llc-board-of-managers)
    *Cited for:* structuring a corporate-style board inside an LLC operating agreement.

## E. Case studies

42. **Zipcar (Chase/Danielson).** Ries, E., "Founder's Dilemmas: Equity Splits," Startup Lessons Learned (2012). [Article](http://www.startuplessonslearned.com/2012/04/founders-dilemmas-equity-splits.html)
    *Cited for:* the canonical 50/50 handshake-before-commitment failure; "a really stupid handshake."

43. **Facebook/Saverin.** Widely documented litigation (settled). *Cited for:* the cost of undefined commitment levels and unilateral dilution; fiduciary-duty exposure.

44. **Snap/Reggie Brown.** TechCrunch (2017): $157.5M settlement disclosed in Snap's IPO filing. [Article](https://techcrunch.com/2017/02/02/snapchat-reggie-brown/)
    *Cited for:* execute IP assignments and founder agreements before value exists.
