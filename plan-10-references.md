# plan10 — Annotated Bibliography

Every source cited in `plan-10-design.md` and `plan-10-operating-agreement.md`, keyed as **[A#]** academic, **[S#]** industry, **[L#]** legal.

**Ratings:** ★★★★ causal/meta-analytic or primary statute/case text · ★★★ large-N correlational, authoritative survey, or well-corroborated case holding · ★★ practitioner framework / proprietary-data summary / law-firm analysis · ★ opinion, single-source, or citation not fully verified this project.

**Verification caveat (applies throughout):** research was done from a sandboxed environment; several publishers and data providers (informs.org, nber.org, carta.com, techstars.com, fi.co, saastr.com, some statute pages) blocked full-text fetches (HTTP 403). All sources below were verified to exist via search-index confirmation against publisher/repository pages; where a specific number rests on snippets or secondary coverage rather than the full primary text, the rating is capped and the entry says so.

## A. Peer-reviewed and academic sources

**[A1] Hellmann, T. & Wasserman, N. (2017). "The First Deal: The Division of Founder Equity in New Ventures." *Management Science* 63(8): 2647–2666.** — https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2474 (also NBER w16922; SSRN 1805427)
Survey of 1,476 founders in 511 US ventures. ~⅓ split equally; quick (≤1 day) equal splits associated with significantly lower first-financing valuations (~10% of equity, ~$450K NPV at stake); effect operates through selection, not causation. **Supports:** design §1.1 (why not a static renegotiation), §11.1. **Rating: ★★★** (large-N correlational; explicitly not causal; numbers partly from abstract/snippets — publisher PDF blocked).

**[A1-WP] Hellmann, T. & Wasserman, N., HBS Working Paper 14-085 (earlier version of A1).** — https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/14-085_2bd67a49-bd41-4396-a69d-73a7f40829b8.pdf
Discusses why founder splits are rarely renegotiated and calls for research on contingent agreements. **Supports:** §11.5 (retroactive renegotiation has no direct literature). **Rating: ★★★** (working paper, full text accessible).

**[A2] Wasserman, N. (2012). *The Founder's Dilemmas.* Princeton University Press.** — https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691158303/the-founders-dilemmas
~10,000-founder CompStudy dataset: 73% of teams split within a month of founding; rich-vs-king control tradeoff; founder-conflict prevalence. **Supports:** §1.1, §3.1. **Rating: ★★★** (large-N descriptive, book format; companion analyses peer-reviewed).

**[A3] Hennicke, M. & Mueller, E. (2022). "Unequal implies success? How initial ownership split impacts team entry and new venture performance." *Economic Modelling*.** — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0263237322001785
24,194 German multi-founder startups (registry data): unequal splits → ~1.3pp higher 3-year employment growth; equal-split teams less likely to admit new members/investors. **Supports:** §1.1 (replication outside US survey data; equal splits correlate with rigidity). **Rating: ★★★** (large-N correlational).

**[A4] Mueller, E. (2026). "Promoting novelty creation: The role of ownership distribution in new venture teams." *Strategic Entrepreneurship Journal* 20(2): 325–354.** — https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sej.70014
5,114 inventive projects, 2,148 German firms: unequal splits with a majority owner on the team associate with higher novelty. **Supports:** background to §3 (concentrated ownership aids decisiveness). **Rating: ★★★** (large-N correlational).

**[A5] FLAGGED — Bapna & Ganco.** The only verifiable Bapna & Ganco paper (*Management Science* 67(5): 2679–2710) is a gender-in-crowdfunding RCT, **not** an equity-split study. Deliberately not cited for splits. **Rating: n/a** (listed to document the negative check).

**[A6] Kagan, E., Leider, S. & Lovejoy, W. S. (2020). "Equity Contracts and Incentive Design in Start-Up Teams." *Management Science* 66(10): 4879–4898.** — https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3439
Lab experiment with real product-building dyads: the equal-split penalty is driven by **sorting** (low contributors select equal splits); **delaying contracting until contribution is observable mitigates it**. **Supports:** §1.1 (the load-bearing causal result for a contribution-measured, late-settled allocation), §5, §11.3/§11.5. **Rating: ★★★★** (causal-lab; dyad-N not confirmable — publisher PDF blocked; external-validity caveat).

**[A7] Hellmann, T. & Thiele, V. (2015). "Contracting Among Founders." *Journal of Law, Economics, & Organization* 31(3): 629–661.** — https://academic.oup.com/jleo/article-abstract/31/3/629/808939
Formal theory: vesting/contingent contracts mitigate team-formation inefficiencies; delay attractive under skill uncertainty. **Supports:** §5 (theoretical grounding of vesting), §11.4. **Rating: ★★★** (formal theory, no data).

**[A8] Negative finding: no peer-reviewed empirical evaluation of any dynamic-equity framework (Slicing Pie or otherwise) could be located.** **Supports:** §1.1 caveat, §11.3 — the honesty constraint on the whole design. **Rating: n/a** (absence-of-evidence finding; searched this project).

**[A9] Breugst, N., Patzelt, H. & Rathgeber, P. (2015). "How should we divide the pie? Equity distribution and its impact on entrepreneurial teams." *Journal of Business Venturing* 30(1): 66–94.** — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0883902614000676
8 teams over ~6 months: **perceived justice**, not equality, drives positive vs negative team spirals. **Supports:** §1.1, §3.3, §4.2. **Rating: ★★** (small-N qualitative, theory-building).

**[A10] Colquitt, J. A. et al. (2001). "Justice at the Millennium." *Journal of Applied Psychology* 86(3): 425–445.** — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11419803/
Meta-analysis, 183 studies: procedural justice independently predicts system-level trust and commitment; distributive justice predicts outcome satisfaction. **Supports:** §4.2, §4.5 (procedure buffers unequal outcomes). **Rating: ★★★★** (meta-analytic; employee samples, extrapolated to founders).

**[A11] Shaw, J. D. (2014). "Pay Dispersion." *Annual Review of Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior* 1: 521–544.** — https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-orgpsych-031413-091253
Authoritative review: dispersion **explained by legitimate inputs** can help; **unexplained** dispersion damages cooperation, worst under high interdependence. **Supports:** §3.3, §4.2, §7 (the professor's premium must be legible market data). **Rating: ★★★** (narrative review of large-N field studies; pay, not equity).

**[A12] Karau, S. J. & Williams, K. D. (1993). "Social Loafing: A Meta-Analytic Review." *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 65(4): 681–706.** — http://www.psych.purdue.edu/~willia55/392F-'06/KarauWilliamsMetaAnalysisJPSP.pdf
78 studies, overall effect g ≈ 0.44; loafing largely eliminated when individual outputs are identifiable/evaluable. **Supports:** §4.5 (evidence-linked logging, full-ledger transparency). **Rating: ★★★★** (meta-analytic, causal-lab primaries; short-task settings).

**[A13] Backes-Gellner, U., Werner, A. & Mohnen, A. (2015). "Effort provision in entrepreneurial teams." *Journal of Business Economics* 85(3): 205–230.** — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11573-014-0749-x
214 German startup teams: free-riding grows with team size; peer pressure/monitoring offsets. **Supports:** §4.5 (peer verification in real founding teams). **Rating: ★★★** (field correlational, self-reported hours).

**[A14] Wasserman, N. (2017). "The throne vs. the kingdom: Founder control and value creation in startups." *Strategic Management Journal* 38(2): 255–277.** — https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/smj.2478
6,130 US private startups: each added founder-control lever (CEO + board) associated with 17.1–22.0% lower pre-money valuation. **Supports:** §3.1, §3.2 (no entrenchment, no super-voting). **Rating: ★★★** (large-N correlational; endogeneity acknowledged by author).

**[A15] Krause, R., Priem, R. & Love, L. (2015). "Who's in charge here? Co-CEOs, power gaps, and firm performance." *Strategic Management Journal* 36(13): 2099–2110.** — https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/smj.2325
Co-CEO public firms: performance rises with the power gap between co-CEOs up to a threshold (inverted-U); truly equal co-leadership underperforms. **Supports:** §3.1 (single CEO). **Rating: ★★★** (correlational; rare-event sample; exact N unconfirmed — publisher blocked).

**[A16] Feigen, M. A., Jenkins, M. & Warendh, A. (2022). "Is It Time to Consider Co-CEOs?" *Harvard Business Review*, Jul–Aug 2022.** — https://hbr.org/2022/07/is-it-time-to-consider-co-ceos
87 co-CEO public companies: 9.5% vs 6.9% average annual shareholder return; ~60% outperformed. **Supports:** §11.2 — cited as the honest counterpoint to [A15], not as design support. **Rating: ★** (practitioner; no selection controls).

**[A17] Greer, L. L., de Jong, B. A., Schouten, M. E. & Dannals, J. E. (2018). "Why and when hierarchy impacts team effectiveness: A meta-analytic integration." *Journal of Applied Psychology* 103(6): 591–613.** — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29369654/
54 studies, 13,914 teams: hierarchy's net effect slightly negative (perf ρ = −.08); positive only when legitimate and stable. **Supports:** §3.1 synthesis (differentiated-but-legitimate authority), §11.2. **Rating: ★★★★** (meta-analytic).

**[A18] Lee, S. (2022). "The myth of the flat start-up." *Strategic Management Journal* 43(1): 58–92.** — https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/smj.3333
US video-game startups: flat structures → more novelty but worse commercial performance and survival. **Supports:** §3.1. **Rating: ★★★** (large-N, single-industry correlational).

**[A19] Gompers, P., Gornall, W., Kaplan, S. & Strebulaev, I. (2020). "How do venture capitalists make decisions?" *Journal of Financial Economics* 135(1): 169–190.** — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X19301680
885 institutional VCs: management team cited as important by 95%, the single most important factor by 47%. **Supports:** §1, §3.1 (team quality/governance is what investors buy). **Rating: ★★★** (large-N stated-preference survey).

**[A20] Ewens, M. & Marx, M. (2018). "Founder Replacement and Startup Performance." *Review of Financial Studies* 31(4): 1532–1565.** — https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/31/4/1532/4604800
Instrumented (noncompete-law changes, 14 states): founder replacement **causally improves** startup performance. **Supports:** §3.1, §3.2 (removability/control migration as value creation). **Rating: ★★★★** (quasi-causal IV; strongest identification cited in this project).

**[A21] Engelen, A. et al. (2026). "The dynamics of narcissism in founding teams." *Journal of Business Venturing* 41(2).** — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0883902625000977
911 US tech ventures over 10 years: documents high base rates and consequences of co-founder turnover. **Supports:** §6 (departure mechanics will very likely be used). Note: the popular "65% lose a co-founder before Series B" stat is *not* peer-reviewed. **Rating: ★★★** (longitudinal correlational; full author list unconfirmed).

**[A22] Negative finding: no peer-reviewed causal study of founder vesting on outcomes exists.** Support for vesting = theory [A7] + lab [A6] + adoption data [S6][S7]. **Supports:** §5, §11.4. **Rating: n/a** (absence-of-evidence finding).

**[A23] Weissenböck, E., Breugst, N., Patzelt, H. & Dibbern, R. (2024). "Equity ownership and identification with the founding team." *Applied Psychology* 73(4): 1626–1651.** — https://iaap-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/apps.12515
82 founders, longitudinal: curvilinear link between stake size and team identification — stake changes have psychological, not just financial, effects. **Supports:** §6 (why inactive founders keep earned stakes). **Rating: ★★** (small-N longitudinal).

**[A24] Wasserman's "65% of high-potential startup failures trace to people problems"** — from the CompStudy dataset, transmitted via industry secondaries (Entrepreneur 2021: https://www.entrepreneur.com/leadership/harvard-business-school-professor-says-65-of-startups-fail/370367; CNN Money 2014). **Supports:** §1 framing. **Rating: ★★** (academic data via secondary reporting; primary table not verified).

## L. Legal and tax authorities (business-terms level; counsel to verify all before drafting)

**[L1] M.G.L. c. 156C (Massachusetts LLC Act), esp. §§21, 24–26, 63.** — https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXII/Chapter156C/Section24 ; https://law.justia.com/codes/massachusetts/part-i/title-xxii/chapter-156c/section-63/

**[L2] *Donahue v. Rodd Electrotype*, 367 Mass. 578 (1975).** — https://law.justia.com/cases/massachusetts/supreme-court/1975/367-mass-578-2.html

**[L3] *Wilkes v. Springside Nursing Home*, 370 Mass. 842 (1976).** — https://law.justia.com/cases/massachusetts/supreme-court/1976/370-mass-842-2.html

**[L4] *Allison v. Eriksson*, 479 Mass. 626 (2018).** — https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/spr-jud-crt-mas-suf/1896955.html
