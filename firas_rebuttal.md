# Cofounder Ownership and Investor Expectations

**Memo to:** The Founding Team
**From:** Firas
**Re:** Why the cofounders' combined ownership should be secured above 50% before we raise
**Date:** August 2026

---

## The situation

Under the current plan, the four cofounders are guaranteed only a **20% base — 5% each**. Everything beyond that must be earned from the shared contribution pool, where cofounders and employees compete under the same rules.

Our 20% *may* grow well past 50% through that pool — if cofounders contribute the most hours, it will. **But it is not guaranteed.** The final number is an output of a work ledger, not a term of the agreement. Depending on who logs what, the cofounders could end the ledger at 60% — or at 40%. Nobody can promise which today.

## Why this matters to investors

- Across 45,000+ startups, the **median founding team owns 56.2% after the seed round** ([Carta Founder Ownership Report](https://carta.com/data/founder-ownership-2026/)). Guidance for entering a seed round is founders at [~70%](https://www.equitylist.co/blog-post/founder-ownership-by-round), and [at least ~50% through the round](https://www.crv.com/content/startup-equity-structure).
- Ownership keeps shrinking after seed: the median team falls to **36% after Series A and 23% after Series B** ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/founder-ownership-2026/)). A team that *starts* the ladder at 40% reaches Series B at ~16% — thin enough to raise the founder-motivation question in every later round.
- The reason investors care is motivation: they are underwriting **7–10 more years of founder commitment**, and the evidence says operators with large personal stakes perform better ([Jensen & Meckling 1976](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304405X7690026X); [Fahlenbrach 2009](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v16y2009i1p136-150.html)). Founders below investors' combined ownership is a recognized diligence red flag ([Equity Matrix](https://equitymatrix.io/blog/what-investors-look-for-in-cap-tables)).
- The largest academic study of founder equity ([Hellmann & Wasserman, *Management Science* 2016](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2474)) adds two findings: badly structured splits are associated with a **~10% lower company value at first financing**, and renegotiating a split after signing rarely succeeds. Whatever we want the number to be, we must set it **before** we sign.

## The proposal

Add a **cofounder-only contribution pool of 35–40%** alongside the 20% base:

| Layer | Size | How it's earned |
|---|---|---|
| Cofounder base (4 × 5%) | 20% | Vests over time while active |
| Cofounder contribution pool | 35–40% | Earned by contribution, competed among cofounders only |
| **Cofounders combined** | **55–60% — guaranteed by structure** | |

The remaining ~40–45% serves employees and future hires, earned under the same contribution rules as today.

Nothing about the plan's philosophy changes: every share is still earned, nothing is granted, and inactivity still costs you. The only change is that the *class split* between cofounders and everyone else becomes a **decision we make**, instead of an accident we discover — and the decision puts the cofounders where funded companies actually sit when they raise.

## The ask

Agree the cofounder-only pool (35–40%) before we ratify the equity plan. It costs nothing if the ledger would have landed us above 50% anyway — and it is the difference between a fundable cap table and a broken one if it wouldn't.
